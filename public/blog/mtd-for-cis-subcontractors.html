Making Tax Digital, CIS

MTD for CIS subcontractors: what changed in April 2026, and what is coming for you

10 August 2026, 11 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • MTD for Income Tax is live: over 50,000 pounds of gross income means digital records and four quarterly updates.
  • HMRC counts what you invoiced, not what you banked, so CIS deductions do not lower the measure.
  • The 30,000 pound wave lands April 2027 and catches most full-time subbies.
  • Nothing about your tax bill changes. The shape of the year does.

For as long as anyone on site can remember, self-employed tax worked one way: keep your paperwork loosely, do a tax return in January grudgingly, argue about the bill annually. That era is ending. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax went live in April 2026, replacing the once-a-year scramble with something HMRC likes much better: a running commentary on your business, four times a year, straight from software.

If you are a CIS subcontractor, this lands on you specifically. Here is the whole picture: what is live now, what is coming, and the one detail about CIS that catches nearly everyone out.

What actually changed in April 2026

From 6 April 2026, sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over 50,000 pounds must keep digital records in software, send HMRC a quarterly summary of income and expenses through recognised software, and file a final declaration that replaces the old Self Assessment ritual. Spreadsheets on their own do not cut it, and paper certainly does not.

The CIS trap: it is your gross that counts

The threshold is measured on your gross qualifying income, before CIS deductions. Invoice 55,000 and take home 44,000 after the 20 percent deduction? HMRC counts the 55,000. You are in.

This is the single most misunderstood point on site. Subbies doing ordinary work, invoicing 4,500 a month, assume they are nowhere near the line because the bank only ever sees the after-deduction figure. HMRC does not consult your bank balance. It counts what you invoiced.

The timetable: three waves

  • April 2026, over 50,000. Live now. If this is you and you are not filing quarterly, you are already in the regime and the clock is unkind.
  • April 2027, over 30,000. The big one for construction. Most full-time subbies clear 30k gross without trying.
  • April 2028, over 20,000. By this point it is essentially everyone self-employed bar part-timers.

Not sure which wave is yours? The ten-second checker will tell you. No email address required.

Four tax returns a year? No. But also, sort of

Let us be precise, because the bloke in the next van will not be. A quarterly update is not a full tax return. It is a summary of the quarter's income and expenses: no tax calculation to agonise over, no payment triggered by the update itself. The final declaration at year end settles the actual position.

But a quarterly update is only painless if your records are already digital and already right. If receipts live in the glovebox, every quarter becomes a mini January. Four panics a year instead of one. That is the real change: MTD punishes bad record keeping four times as often.

Penalties: the points ratchet

Miss quarterly deadlines and HMRC's points-based system starts counting. Each late submission earns a point; at the threshold, fixed penalties begin, with interest riding on any late payment. It is designed like driving licence points: forgiving of a one-off, expensive as a habit. The cheapest number of points is zero, and the way to hold zero is to make the filings someone else's job.

The upside nobody mentions

Quarterly digital records are genuinely good for you. For the first time you see profit during the year, not eleven months after the fact. CIS refunds, the annual mystery cheque, become predictable because deductions are tracked as they happen. And when you want a van on finance or a mortgage, your figures are current instead of a year stale.

What to do now, by wave

  • Over 50k: you are live. Get recognised software and a process this month, or hand the lot over and be filing by next quarter.
  • 30k to 50k: your start date is April 2027. The smart move is clean digital records from now, so day one is a non-event. The Solo waitlist locks a 59 pound founding rate.
  • 20k to 30k: you have until April 2028, same logic, less urgency.

What a year on MTD actually looks like

  • 6 Apr to 5 Jul
    Quarter one

    Records kept as you go. Update due 7 August.

  • 6 Jul to 5 Oct
    Quarter two

    Update due 7 November. Figures are cumulative, so quarter one errors wash out here.

  • 6 Oct to 5 Jan
    Quarter three

    Update due 7 February, straight after the busiest fortnight of the tax calendar.

  • 6 Jan to 5 Apr
    Quarter four

    Update due 7 May. The year is now fully reported.

  • By 31 January
    Final declaration

    Adjustments, reliefs, other income, and the payment. Same date it always was.

Four postcards and one proper return. No tax is calculated at the quarterly points.

What changes

  • Records must be digital from the moment of purchase
  • Four summaries a year, from recognised software
  • A final declaration replaces the SA return
  • Penalty points for late updates

What does not change

  • How much tax you owe
  • 31 January and 31 July payment dates
  • How CIS deductions are credited
  • Your allowable expenses

The CIS interaction, in detail

Set your software up to record income gross, with the deduction shown separately as tax paid on account. Get this wrong and two things break at once: HMRC sees understated income, which is a compliance problem, and you see understated earnings, which is a business problem. Every decent package has a CIS switch; turn it on before the first invoice rather than reconstructing later.

The four habits that make MTD painless as a subbie

  • Record gross, show the deduction separately, always
  • Collect deduction statements monthly and store them against the month
  • Split materials on every invoice so deductions only touch labour
  • File each quarter on time even if a figure is still an estimate: it is correctable

MTD does not audit you four times a year. It just stops you finding out in January what happened in May.
