Guide 05 · Reference

Every screen, every button.

The complete reference for app.klyara.com — what each screen is for, what every control does, what the software works out for you, and what it will not let you do. Guides 03 and 04 teach; this one answers.

Who this is for. Anyone answering a question about the app: Key, Craig, a new starter in their first week, or a client on the phone. Find the screen, read the row.

How it is laid out. Part 1 is the client app, screen by screen. Part 2 is the admin side. Part 3 is what the database refuses to do regardless of what anybody clicks. Part 4 is every rate and threshold the app uses, with the source. Part 5 is what to do when something looks wrong.

How the app is arranged

One web app, two faces. A client sees their own business and nothing else. An admin sees every client. Which one you get is decided by your role in the database, not by the address you type — there is no separate admin login to leak.

WhereWhat you get
PhoneFive tabs along the bottom — Home, Jobs, Receipts, Invoices, More. Everything else lives behind More. The camera button on Receipts is deliberately the biggest target on the screen.
Tablet and desktopA full sidebar with every screen listed. Same app, same data, more room.
InstalledAdd to Home Screen turns it into an app icon with no browser chrome. It is the same web app — nothing to update, nothing from an app store.
OfflineScreens you have already opened still load. Anything that needs the server will wait and tell you, rather than silently failing.

The client app

The answer to “what do I need to know this morning”, and nothing else.

Jobs

The spine of the whole app. Receipts, mileage, expenses, quotes and invoices all point at a job, which is how the app can answer the one question accounting software cannot: did this job make money?

ControlWhat it does
New jobName, your own reference, site address, who is paying, and the quoted total if you have one. Only the name is required.
StatusQuoting Won Active Done Lost. Setting a job to Active stamps the start date; Done stamps the finish date. Both can be corrected.
Job detailMoney in, money out and margin for that job alone, then every receipt, trip, expense and invoice attached to it.
MarginInvoiced less costs. Costs are receipts plus expenses plus the mileage claim. It is a working figure, not a set of accounts.

Why job_id is optional everywhere

A receipt photographed at a petrol station does not always belong to a job, and forcing a choice at that moment would kill the one-tap flow that makes the product work. Tag it later from the receipt, or never.

Receipts

The screen that is the product. Photograph, done.

  1. Tap the camera

    It opens the phone camera straight away. No menu, no form first.

  2. Take the picture

    The image is compressed in the browser before it leaves the phone — usually to about 300 KB — so it works on a bad signal in a van.

  3. Add what you know, or nothing

    Amount, supplier, a note, a job. All optional. An untagged photo is still worth more than a lost one.

StatusMeaning
NewWith us. Nothing for you to do.
ApprovedChecked and accepted, about to go into your books.
SentFiled into QuickBooks, Xero or FreeAgent. Finished.
RejectedWe could not use it — the reason is on the receipt. Usually blurred, cut off, or a duplicate. Photograph it again.

The one rule

A client can never move their own receipt through the workflow. Only an admin can approve, send or reject. This is enforced in the database, not in the screen — see Part 3.

Invoices and quotes

Same shape, one table, told apart by a kind. Accepting a quote copies it into a new invoice rather than changing it, because what was quoted has to stay provable afterwards.

ControlWhat it does
NumberFilled in for you — INV0001, Q0001 — sequential per business. Gaps are allowed (a deleted draft leaves one). Numbers are never reused, which is the part HMRC cares about.
Line kindLabour, Materials, Plant, Other. This is not decoration: CIS is deducted from labour only, so the split decides the money.
Quantity × unit priceA negative unit price is allowed, for a credit line or a retention deduction. The quantity must be positive.
VAT treatmentStandard 20%, Reduced 5%, Zero, Reverse charge, or Not registered. Defaults from what we already know about the business.
Reverse charge rateAppears only when reverse charge is chosen. The customer accounts for this rate themselves; it is shown on the invoice because HMRC requires it, and it is never added to the total.
CIS deductionNone, 20% registered, or 30% not verified. Applied to the labour lines and to the net figure, never to the VAT.
StateDraft Sent Paid Accepted Void. Only a draft can be edited. Void rather than delete.
Print or save as PDFThe invoice is styled for print — the navigation and buttons disappear. Save as PDF and email it.

Reverse charge is not automatic

The app suggests it when the business is in the CIS chain, but it does not apply where the customer has told you in writing that they are an end user or an intermediary — a landlord, a developer selling the finished building, or a householder. No written statement, no reverse charge: charge VAT and ask them for one.

Mileage

Money most builders throw away. Thirty miles to a merchant and back is £16.50 off the tax bill at the 2026/27 rate, and logging it takes ten seconds.

On screenWhat it is
Miles this yearBusiness miles in the current tax year, 6 April to 5 April.
WorthWhat those miles take off taxable profit.
Rate nowThe rate the next mile earns, and how many are left before the step down.
Log a tripDate, from, to, miles, job, what for. The value is worked out from the date travelled, not today.

Two things this gets right that most apps do not

The rate follows the date. HMRC raised the first-10,000 rate from 45p to 55p on 6 April 2026. A trip logged for last March is still valued at 45p, because that is what it was worth.

The step is applied inside the trip. With 9,990 business miles already logged, a 500-mile trip is 10 miles at 55p and 490 at 25p — £128.00, not £275.00. The database works this out, so it is right regardless of what the phone sends.

Expenses

For money spent without a receipt to photograph — a direct debit, a subscription, an insurance premium. Category, amount, VAT if you know it, and a job if it belongs to one.

Weekends and bank holidays

HMRC does not accept a payment late because the due date fell on a Sunday. Every date is pulled back to the last working day before it, and English and Welsh bank holidays are computed — including Easter and the substitute days when Christmas lands on a weekend — rather than typed into a list that expires.

Tools

Eight calculators, each opening with this business's own figures already in it. A blank calculator is a chore; one that knows your turnover and your CIS status answers the question before you have typed anything.

ToolAnswersThe part people get wrong
CIS deductionWhat comes off, in both directions.The deduction never touches materials, plant you hired in, or the VAT. It does touch plant you own, because HMRC treats that as part of what you are charging (CISR15090).
VAT and reverse chargeAdd it, take it out, or work out whether you charge it at all.Net, VAT and gross always add up exactly — rounding each separately loses a penny, and that is what gets a return queried.
VAT thresholdHow close you are to having to register.HMRC tests a rolling twelve months, not a tax year. Register within 30 days of the end of the month you go over.
Day rate builderWhat you actually need to charge.220 working days, not 260. Pricing on 260 assumes you never take a day off and it never rains.
Late payment interestWhat the law entitles you to on an overdue commercial debt.Base rate plus 8%, and the base rate is the one in force on the 31 December or 30 June before the debt fell due — fixed for that whole six months. Plus fixed compensation of £40, £70 or £100.
Subbie take-homeDay rate to money in your pocket.Above £100,000 the personal allowance is withdrawn £1 for every £2, so that slice is effectively taxed at 60%. Above £125,140 the rate is 45%.
True cost of an employeeWhat someone really costs against a subbie day rate.The pension minimum is 3% of qualifying earnings — the slice between £6,240 and £50,270 — not 3% of the salary. And the £10,500 Employment Allowance offsets employer NIC for most employers, though not a sole director with no other staff.
Flat rate or standardWhich VAT scheme wins.Two things it cannot know: whether you are a limited cost business (which forces 16.5% and usually kills it), and whether most of your work is reverse charge (which almost always kills it).

What these are not

They give a good answer fast. They are not tax advice for one specific situation. If a number here changes what someone is about to do, they should ask us first — and the screen says so.

Where you are

Money in, money out and profit by month, then which jobs actually paid. Exports to a spreadsheet with a UTF-8 byte order mark so Excel opens it correctly, and with any cell that starts with =, +, - or @ made inert so a supplier name can never execute as a formula.

Documents

Everything we publish: accounts, VAT returns, CIS statements, letters. Read-only — a client cannot publish a document to themselves, which is what makes the folder trustworthy. Files are held in a private bucket and reached by a signed link that expires.

Messages

One thread per business, both sides in one place, so this stops living on WhatsApp and survives a lost phone. Anything from us is marked as read as soon as it is on screen. A client cannot send a message that looks like it came from Klyara.

Add-ons

Things we can take off their hands. Asking raises an enquiry; we come back with a price. There is no checkout and nothing is charged from this screen.

Your account

Name, phone, email, password, and the business details a client is allowed to correct themselves: company name, VAT registration, CIS status, VAT stagger, year end, MTD and payroll. Tier, platform, status, the receipt inbox address and the onboarding checklist are all admin-only.

Rules that do not bend

A receipt is never approved without being read. A rejection always carries a reason the client can act on. The inbox address is set once, from the platform, and checked before the first receipt is routed. Nothing identifying a client goes anywhere outside the portal.

Part 3

What the software refuses to do

The browser talks to the database directly, so the browser is not trusted. Every rule below lives in Postgres and holds no matter what anyone types into a console.

AttemptResult
See another business's anythingBlocked. Row-level security on every table, and both reporting views run as the caller rather than as their owner.
Make yourself an adminBlocked. The role column is pinned on update.
Give yourself a better tier, or mark yourself activeBlocked on update and on insert. Signup writes a row; it does not get to decide what that row is worth.
Tick your own AML identity checkBlocked. The onboarding checklist is forced to all-false at signup and admin-only afterwards.
Redirect your receipt inboxBlocked. That address is admin-only, and is where approved receipts are emailed.
Approve your own receiptBlocked. Only an admin can move the status.
Point a receipt at someone else's folderBlocked. The stored path must sit inside the client's own folder; .. and absolute paths are rejected outright.
Publish a document to yourselfBlocked. Publishing is admin-only.
Send a message that looks like it came from KlyaraBlocked.
Zero out an invoice total, or invent oneBlocked. Every money column is computed from the lines by the database and pinned against direct writes.
Invent a mileage rateBlocked. The database prices the trip from the date travelled and ignores what the browser sent.
File an add-on enquiry already marked wonBlocked. It always arrives as new.

All of the above are tested against the live database by scripts/rls-test.mjs, which creates throwaway users, tries every one of them, and deletes everything it made. Every line must read PASS before a real client is added.

Part 5

When something looks wrong

SymptomAlmost always
A screen says “We cannot find that one”The record was deleted or the link is old. It is not a crash — go back to the list.
A screen shows a setup message instead of the appThe build went out without its environment variables. Rebuild; the build now refuses rather than shipping an empty bundle.
A client says they cannot see a documentCheck it was published to their client record. Publishing is per business.
An invoice total looks wrongCheck the line kinds. CIS comes off labour only, so a labour line typed as materials changes the money.
A deadline looks a day earlyThat is deliberate. Dates are pulled back off weekends and bank holidays to the last working day.
A mileage claim looks lowCheck the total for the tax year. Past 10,000 miles the rate drops to 25p, and a trip that crosses the line is split.
Nothing loads at allCheck app.klyara.com in a browser and then the Supabase project. The app is two things: static files on Cloudflare, and a database. One of them is down.

The tests that prove all of this. Run from the portal folder:

  • node scripts/test-tax.ts — every rate and date rule, 61 assertions.
  • node scripts/verify-db.mjs — the live schema: tables, policies, triggers, grants, views.
  • node scripts/rls-test.mjs — 37 real attacks against the live database.
  • node scripts/smoke-live.mjs https://app.klyara.com — the deployed site, signed in as a client and as an admin.
  • node uitest2.mjs, node a11y.mjs, node cspall.mjs — the screens themselves, against a local build.
