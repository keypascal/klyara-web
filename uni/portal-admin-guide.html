Guide 04 · For Craig and whoever runs the desk

Running the portal.

The daily routine is two minutes. Everything else in here is for the handful of times a month when it is not.

The shape of it. Clients photograph receipts. You look at a queue, approve or send back. Approved receipts are emailed straight into that client's accounting platform, and their status flips to "In your books". You publish documents when you have them. That is the whole job.

The morning routine

  1. Open the Queue

    Admin → Queue. Oldest first, so nobody waits longer than anybody else.

  2. For each one: read it, type the supplier and the amount, Approve

    Supplier and amount are optional but take three seconds and save you looking the receipt up again later.

  3. Anything you cannot read: Send back

    Pick one of the four canned reasons or write your own. The client sees your words exactly as you type them, so keep it kind — they are up a ladder, not being careless.

  4. Empty queue, done

    If it takes more than two minutes per client per day, something about the design is wrong and it is worth saying so.

Taking on a new client

They sign up themselves; you finish the setup. Open Admin → Clients → their name.

When all five are ticked a Mark as active button appears. Press it and their portal stops saying "getting you set up".

The receipt inbox address — the one field that matters

This is where approved receipts get emailed. Until it is set, receipts approve fine but cannot be filed, and the queue shows a no inbox set warning.

PlatformWhere to find the address
QuickBooksBanking → Receipts → the "Send receipts to" address.
XeroBusiness → Files → the email-to-Files address for that organisation.
FreeAgentBills → the auto-extract inbox address.

Why this design is worth protecting

Moving a client onto a different back end — Dext, a different platform, anything — is changing this one email address. The client sees nothing. No migration, no retraining, no conversation. Do not let anyone talk you into hard-wiring a platform integration in place of it.

Publishing a document

Admin → Publish. Pick the client, pick the kind, give it a title in the words you would use out loud — "Management figures — August", not "MGMT_ACCS_AUG26_FINAL_v3". Add a period, choose the file, publish. They get a notification straight away.

Enquiries

When a client asks about an add-on it lands here as new. Move it along as you go: contacted, won, dismissed. These are people who have already bought from you once, so they are the easiest revenue on the list — but the pipeline only works if you actually move the cards.

The control panel

Admin → Control. The same plan, business plan, document library, compliance board and launch view that used to live at klyara.com/admin.html — now behind a real login rather than a password typed into a static page.

It reads the same data files as the old panel, so the two cannot drift. The old page still works and will keep working until you are happy to retire it.

"The portal cannot reach its database"A deploy went out without its settings.Cloudflare Pages → the portal project → Settings → Environment variables. Both Supabase values must be set, then redeploy.
A client sees someone else's dataStop. This is the serious one. Ring Key immediately. Do not add another client until it is understood.

The rules that do not bend

Read this once a quarter

  • Never paste a service key, a database password or an API token into the portal, a chat, or a document. They belong in Supabase secrets and the Cloudflare dashboard, nowhere else.
  • Nobody gets made an admin casually. An admin sees every client's receipts and every client's documents. It is Key, it is you, and it is nobody else without a conversation first.
  • Do not tick the ID check until you have seen the ID. That checkbox is an AML record. It is the one thing in here that could cost the practice its licence.
  • Client files stay in the portal. Not in email, not on a desktop, not in a WhatsApp thread. One place, one copy, one retention schedule.
  • Nothing sensitive goes in the Uni or the website repository. Everything there is publicly readable by anyone who guesses the address.

What the client sees

Worth keeping in your head when you write a rejection reason or a document title: this is the whole of their experience. A greeting, what is outstanding, and two numbers. If what you type does not make sense on that screen, it does not make sense.

The client's own guide is guide 03 — send them that rather than explaining it down the phone.
