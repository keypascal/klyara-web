# BLOCK 2 — The compliance chain (20 min) 🔴

**This block gates revenue. Nothing else in the plan matters until it's resolved.**

### 8. ICB or AAT — which route?
**My recommendation: ICB.** £94 application + £206/year, **AML supervision included**, 28-day
processing, plus a professional body, public directory, discounted PII and engagement letter
templates. Registering with HMRC direct is £300 up front, £400 per premises annually, up to 45 days,
and gets you supervision and nothing else.

**But** — it depends entirely on Craig's qualifications and which body he can join. That's the
question. *(Detail and sources in `docs/17 §1`.)*

### 9. Has the application actually gone in, and on what date?
Every "when can we take clients" decision keys off this date. If it hasn't gone in, it goes in the
day after our session.

### 10. What can Klyara legally do *today*, before supervision?
The pack assumes: education, waitlist, deposits **no**, bookkeeping by way of business **no**. I want
that confirmed rather than assumed, because ~40 pieces of content are written in two versions and I
need to know which one ships.

### 11. PII insurance — arranged, and at what cover level?
It's a condition of the ICB licence. Also the thing that lets you sleep.

### 12. Who is the AML-nominated officer, and is the firm-wide risk assessment written?
MLR 2017 requires both. `docs/12` assumes they exist by client one.

### 13. Continuity arrangement — who covers if Craig is hit by a bus?
Required by most professional bodies, and it's also the answer to the single biggest risk in this
whole business. The accountant partnership channel in `docs/11 §8` is the natural place to solve it.

# BLOCK 3 — Money (20 min)

### 14. What is the actual monthly budget, and what's the runway?
The pack assumes roughly £180–245/month fixed cost plus ~£25 for a scheduler. I've held every
recommendation inside that. If the real number is different, the plan changes — particularly ads.

### 15. Google Ads: how much, and when?
Currently written and paused. The £500 credit is a **match offer** — you spend ~£400 to get £500.
My recommendation: don't switch on until AML lands, then £300/month with a hard £40/booked-call kill
rule, reviewed at £150 spent.

### 16. Is the pricing final?
£59 / £99 / £199 / £329 plus onboarding. **Neither Guildford construction-specialist competitor
publishes pricing at all** (`docs/17 §5`), so publishing yours is a real edge — but it also means
there's no local benchmark. Worth one mystery-shop enquiry to see where £199 actually sits.

### 17. The founding rate — what exactly are you promising?
"First ten firms, rate held for as long as they stay" is in the content. That's a permanent discount
on your first ten clients. **Is that genuinely what you want?** An alternative: rate held for 24
months, then standard. Say now, because it's in writing the moment we launch.

### 18. Late-records surcharge — £25 after the 10th. Will you actually charge it?
A surcharge you never apply is worse than none, because it teaches people the deadline is soft.

# BLOCK 7 — Pricing decisions (added after the market benchmark)

*Fifteen minutes. All six come out of `docs/22`, which put real published UK prices next to yours
for the first time. Craig not needed for any of these.*

### 25. Do we hold the launch prices, or raise them now?

My recommendation is **hold**, and I would defend it: launching at £59–£329 with no reviews, no case
studies and no licence displayed yet means price is currently doing a real job — it is buying the
first ten clients, and those are worth more as proof than as revenue.

But the market data says Solo at £59 is **below the published price of the CIS return on its own**
(£80–£200/month standalone, from £95 at the nearest specialist), and Trade at £199 is a third to two
thirds under the direct comparator (£300–£500 for a contractor-with-subbies bundle).

**What I need:** a yes to hold, or a decision to move now. If we hold, I need agreement to 26 and 27,
because without them the low price becomes permanent by default.

### 26. Can I add "founding client rate, held for twelve months" to every price we publish?

This is the single cheapest decision in the whole pack. It costs nothing today and it is what makes
the increase in September 2027 a scheduled event rather than a confrontation. Without it, every
client who joins in the next twelve months has an implied permanent price.

**What I need:** yes or no. If yes, it goes on the pricing page, the proposal template and the
schedule of services this week.

### 27. Do you accept the September 2027 target prices?

Solo £89 · Start £149 · Trade £279 · Pro £449. Every one of those still sits under the published
comparator, so the position stays *better value than the specialists* rather than *cheapest*.

**What I need:** agreement in principle, so the founding-rate wording has a number behind it. It can
be revised in twelve months with real data — but the review date should go in the diary now.

### 28. What do we charge for the one-off services we are currently giving away?

The nearest specialist charges £400 for a gross payment status application, £250 for reverse charge
setup and from £400 for a company formation. My proposal is £195, £125 and £150 respectively, with
the first two included free on Trade and Pro.

The gross payment status one matters most: it is a defined piece of work worth roughly £24,000 of
cash flow to the client, and it is an excellent first transaction with someone who is not ready to
move their whole bookkeeping yet.

**What I need:** approval of the rate card in `docs/21 §PART 2`, or your changes to it.

### 29. Is 10% of the monthly fee, ongoing, the right partner commission?

On a Trade client that is £19.90 a month, £239 a year, for as long as they stay. An accountant with
four builders sending all four is roughly £950 a year for work they did not want.

The alternative is a one-off finder's fee, which is cheaper and produces one referral instead of a
partner who thinks about you in October. I think ongoing is clearly right, but it is your margin.

**What I need:** the rate confirmed, and agreement to the term that Klyara will not offer year-end
accounts to an introduced client where the partner provides them. That term is what makes the whole
programme sellable and it has to be real.

### 30. Who makes the outbound calls, and when?

`docs/23` assumes twenty cold calls a week in two early-morning blocks, because builders answer at
7:30am and do not answer at 11am. That is the highest-yield hour in the plan and it is the first
thing that gets skipped.

**What I need:** whose diary it goes in, and confirmation that 07:30–08:30 Monday and Wednesday is
actually realistic for that person. If it is not, say so now and we will design around it rather
than have it quietly not happen.
