# 1. Verified compliance facts

These underpin every piece of content in the pack. I checked them at source rather than trusting the

## Making Tax Digital for Income Tax — ✅ verified

| Threshold | Assessed on | Applies from |
|---|---|---|
| Over £50,000 | 2024–25 Self Assessment return | 6 April 2026 |
| Over £30,000 | 2025–26 return | 6 April 2027 |
| Over £20,000 | 2026–27 return | 6 April 2028 |

**The critical one, quoted verbatim from HMRC:** qualifying income is

> "the amount before expenses (also known as turnover), based on the tax return you submitted in the
> previous tax year"

And it combines both income types — HMRC's own worked example: £25,000 rental + £27,000
self-employment = **£52,000 qualifying income**, so in scope.

*Source: [Work out your qualifying income for MTD for Income Tax — GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/work-out-your-qualifying-income-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax) and [Find out if and when you need to use MTD for Income Tax — GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)*

### ⚠️ One precision fix to make across the pack

The pack repeatedly says qualifying income is measured "before CIS deductions". **HMRC's guidance
does not say that in terms** — it says "before expenses (turnover)". The conclusion is still right,
but the reasoning needs stating properly, because a builder who checks will not find those words:

> Turnover on a self-employment return is the gross amount invoiced. CIS deductions are not a
> reduction in turnover — they are recorded separately as tax already paid. So the turnover figure
> HMRC assesses is the pre-CIS number.

**Say it that way from now on:** *"HMRC looks at the turnover box on your return. CIS deductions
don't reduce that box — they go in a different one, as tax you've already paid."* That is accurate,
checkable, and it survives someone pushing back on it.

## AML supervision — ✅ verified, and there is a much better route

**This is the most financially useful finding in this document.**

| Route | Up front | Annual | Processing | AML included |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ICB Practice Licence** | £94 application | **£206** + membership (£167–£200) | **28 days** | ✅ Yes, at no extra cost |
| HMRC direct registration | £300 application | **£400 per premises** | **up to 45 days** | ✅ (it is the supervision) |

HMRC also charges **£40 per person** for approval checks on beneficial owners, officers and managers
in the accountancy sector, and £400 per premises again at annual declaration. There is a small
business reduction — a £500 refund — but only where turnover is **under £5,000**, which will not
apply to Klyara.

**The ICB route is cheaper, faster, and comes with a professional body, a public directory listing,
discounted PII, engagement letter templates and MLR guidance.** The HMRC route gets you supervision
and nothing else.

**Recommendation: go ICB.** Unless Craig's qualifications point at AAT, in which case AAT is also a
statutory supervisor and the same logic applies.

*Sources: [ICB Practice Licence](https://www.bookkeepers.org.uk/Running-a-Practice/Regulation/Practice-Licence) · [ICB fees](https://www.bookkeepers.org.uk/Membership/Fees--Subscriptions) · [Money laundering supervision registration fees — GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/money-laundering-regulations-registration-fees) · [Register or renew your money laundering supervision — GOV.UK](https://gov.uk/guidance/register-or-renew-your-money-laundering-supervision-with-hmrc) · [AAT licensing](https://www.aat.org.uk/membership/standards-requirements/licensing)*

**Note the ICB licence also requires Professional Indemnity Insurance and annual renewal**, and
changes must be notified within 30 days.

## Bank of England base rate — ✅ verified

**3.75%**, held on **30 July 2026**. Next decision **17 September 2026**.

So statutory late payment interest — 8% over base — is currently **11.75%**.

**Re-check this before quoting a number publicly**, particularly after 17 September. The pack says
"8% over base" rather than a fixed percentage everywhere for exactly this reason; keep it that way in
evergreen content and only state 11.75% in dated posts.

*Source: [Bank of England base rate — money.co.uk](https://www.money.co.uk/mortgages/bank-of-england-base-rate)*

## CIS — ⚠️ partially verified

The gov.uk overview page does not carry the operational detail (rates, penalty amounts, statement
deadlines). The figures used throughout the pack — 0/20/30%, the 19th, £100/£200/£300-or-5%, 14 days
for statements — are the long-standing published position and are consistent across the sector, but
I could not pull them from a single authoritative page in this session.

**Action: before publishing any CIS penalty figure, check it against the CIS guide for contractors
and subcontractors (CIS340) on gov.uk.** It is one link and it protects a bookkeeping firm's
credibility on its own specialism.

*Source consulted: [What is the Construction Industry Scheme — GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/what-is-the-construction-industry-scheme)*

# 4. The new angle: AI discovery

**This is the most interesting thing I found and it is not in the pack yet.**

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| **45% of consumers now use AI for local business recommendations — up from 6% a year earlier** | BrightLocal (US) |
| **AI appears in 23% of local search journeys** | BrightLocal (US) |
| **88% verify AI recommendations** by checking the underlying sources | BrightLocal |

A 6% → 45% shift in twelve months is the fastest change in local discovery since mobile. It is US
data and the UK usually trails, but not by much and not for long.

## What actually makes an AI recommend a local business

AI assistants do not have a ranking algorithm you can game. They assemble an answer from sources:
business websites and service pages, Google Business Profile, directories and citations, reviews,
professional credentials, social presence and news mentions. The consistent finding is that
**completeness and consistency beat optimisation** — "AI performs better when information is
complete".

The factors that come up repeatedly: information completeness, identical NAP everywhere, detailed
individual service pages, explicit geographic service areas, FAQ content answering real questions,
structured data, topical depth, and reviews.

*Source: [How ChatGPT Recommends Local Businesses in 2026 — CMC SEO](https://cmc-seo.com/chatgpt-recommends-local-businesses/)*

## The genuinely good news

**You have accidentally already built most of this.** Look at the overlap:

| What AI assistants reward | What the pack already does |
|---|---|
| Detailed individual service pages | ✅ Service pages exist; `docs/08 §9` adds ten more |
| Explicit geographic service areas | ✅ Five local pages with named towns and `areaServed` schema |
| FAQ content answering real questions | ✅ 16 FAQs on the MTD page, 6 per local page, all schema-marked |
| Structured data | ✅ `docs/08` — five schema types |
| Consistent NAP across sources | ✅ Canonical NAP string, 12+ citations in `docs/02` |
| Topical depth on a narrow subject | ✅ Construction-only, 55,000 words of CIS/MTD/VAT content |
| Professional credentials visible | ⏳ Once the ICB licence lands |
| Reviews | ❌ **The gap** |

**Klyara is unusually well positioned to be the answer when someone asks an AI "who does CIS
bookkeeping near Guildford".** Narrow specialism plus deep content plus explicit geography is exactly
the shape these systems reward, and almost no local bookkeeper has it.

## The four things to do about it

1. **Add an "About Klyara" fact block** to the site — a short, plain, unambiguous paragraph stating
   what Klyara does, where, for whom, and from what price. AI systems extract these well. Put it in
   the footer or an About page, in plain text, not in an image.
2. **Get the reviews.** It is the one input you have none of, and it is a named factor across every
   source on this.
3. **Publish the professional credentials the moment they exist.** ICB membership number, supervision
   status, certifications — as text on the page, not a badge image.
4. **Test it monthly.** Ask ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity: *"Who does CIS bookkeeping for
   builders near Guildford?"* Log what comes back. It costs five minutes and it is the only feedback
   loop that exists for this channel.

**Add that test to the Friday production hour from day 30.**

## The four gaps

**1. Nobody publishes prices.** Both construction-specialist competitors use "get a quote" or "book a
consultation". Klyara publishing £59/£99/£199/£329 openly is a genuine, defensible differentiator in
*this specific market* — and it filters out the people who were never going to proceed.

**Do not let anyone talk you into hiding the pricing.** It is your sharpest edge here.

**2. Everyone sells relief, nobody sells competence.** "Reducing your admin burden", "focus on your
business", "friendly meeting". Klyara's angle — *we will tell you the thing you didn't know, on the
first call* — is materially different and it is what the sales script in `docs/09` is built on.

**3. Nobody gives anything away ungated.** Twelve working calculators with no email wall is unmatched
locally and it is what makes the community strategy in `docs/01` possible at all.

**4. Nobody is construction-*only*.** Even the specialists have construction as a division. "That is
all we do" is a claim none of them can make.

## Where they beat you, honestly

- **Trading history and real reviews.** They have them. You do not. This is the whole gap.
- **Full-service.** They do year-end, corporation tax and advisory. You deliberately do not, and some
  prospects want one supplier. The answer is the accountant partnership channel, not scope creep.
- **Established local relationships.** Which is why 20 accountant emails and the Slyfield round matter
  more than another social channel.

# 7. What I could not verify, and what you should check

Being explicit so nothing here is taken on trust.

1. **CIS penalty amounts and statement deadlines** — check CIS340 on gov.uk before publishing figures.
2. **Whether Craig's qualifications route to ICB or AAT** — changes the licence application.
3. **PII insurance cost** — quotes vary by turnover and cover level; ICB members get a discounted route.
4. **Current MTD-compatible software list** — HMRC maintains it and it changes.
5. **Whether the £5,000 small business reduction could ever apply** — almost certainly not, but worth
   thirty seconds before paying the £300 HMRC fee (if you go that route at all, which you shouldn't).
6. **Guildford competitor pricing** — neither publishes. A mystery-shop enquiry would tell you where
   your £199 sits, and it is legitimate market research. Worth doing once.
