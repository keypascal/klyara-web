## The launch date decision

**Start Monday 7 September 2026. Not 31 August.**

31 August 2026 is the Summer Bank Holiday. Half your audience is off, B2B engagement on a bank
holiday Monday is the worst of the year, and you'd spend your single best post — the launch — on a
dead day. The week of 1–4 September is also the school-return week, which is noisy.

7 September also lines the campaign up with something better: **the MTD quarter ending 5 October**,
with its filing deadline of **7 November**. That gives the whole 30 days a real countdown to build
towards instead of a manufactured one.

Day 1 = Mon 7 Sep 2026. Day 30 = Tue 6 Oct 2026.

If you insist on launching 31 August, shift every date in this document back by seven days. Nothing
else changes.

The most expensive four words in construction: "I'll sort it later."

Here's what CIS actually costs when it goes wrong, and I mean in real numbers, not scare stories.

MISSED THE 19th
£100 the day you're late.
£200 at two months.
£300 or 5% of the deductions — whichever is higher — at six months.
Same again at twelve.

That's on a single monthly return. Miss four in a row and you're into four figures on paperwork you

WRONG DEDUCTION RATE
Verify a subbie properly and you deduct 20%. Don't verify, and you're obliged to deduct 30%.

The 10% difference doesn't come out of thin air. If you under-deducted, HMRC can come to YOU for the
shortfall — not the subcontractor. You paid him the money, and you still owe HMRC the difference.

MATERIALS
CIS is deducted on labour, not on materials the subcontractor genuinely paid for. If you're deducting
on the whole invoice because it's simpler, you're taking money off your subbies that isn't yours to
take, and they'll eventually work it out.

If you're not itemising materials on your invoices, you're the subbie losing the money.

If you qualify for gross status and haven't applied, you're handing HMRC an interest-free loan of 20%
of every invoice for up to a year. For a subbie invoicing £120,000, that's £24,000 sat with HMRC
instead of in your account.

The turnover test, the compliance test and the business test are all passable by a well-run firm.
Most people just never apply.

### 🔷 LinkedIn (Craig)
```
The domestic reverse charge has been law since March 2021 and I still see it invoiced wrong every
single week.

Here's the whole thing in one post.

WHEN IT APPLIES — all five must be true:
1. The supply is construction services within the CIS definition
2. Both parties are VAT registered
3. Both parties are registered for CIS
4. The customer is NOT an end user
5. The supply is standard or reduced rated (not zero-rated)

If all five are yes: you don't charge VAT. Your customer accounts for it themselves.

THE BIT PEOPLE MISS — "end user"
If your customer is the one who actually occupies or owns the finished building, they're an end user
and the reverse charge does NOT apply. You charge VAT as normal.

Property developer building to sell? End user.
Main contractor who'll invoice it onward? Not an end user — reverse charge applies.

The customer has to TELL you they're an end user, in writing. Get it in writing. Keep it.

WHAT GOES ON THE INVOICE
No VAT amount charged. A clear statement that the reverse charge applies, and the VAT rate or amount
the customer must account for. Something like:

"Reverse charge: VAT Act 1994 Section 55A applies. Customer to account to HMRC for the reverse
charge output tax on the VAT-exclusive price of items marked 'reverse charge'."

THE CASH FLOW THING NOBODY WARNED ANYONE ABOUT
If most of your sales are now reverse-charged, you're not collecting VAT on them — but you're still
paying VAT on your materials.

That means you may be in a permanent repayment position. If you are, apply to move to MONTHLY VAT
returns. You'll get the money back twelve times a year instead of four.

I've seen firms sit on quarterly returns for two years, wondering why cash is always tight, while
HMRC held four figures of their money for an extra two months at a time.

## DAY 15 — Monday 21 September · ANCHOR

### 🔷 LinkedIn (Craig)
```
You are not a bank. You keep being treated like one.

The average small building firm I look at is owed somewhere between one and two months of turnover at
any given moment. On a firm turning over £400,000, that's £40,000–£70,000 of your money sitting in
someone else's account.

You borrowed to buy the materials. You paid the lads on Friday. And you're waiting.

Here's what you're actually entitled to, because most builders have no idea.

STATUTORY INTEREST — you can charge it, and almost nobody does
Under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts legislation, on a business-to-business debt you can charge
interest at 8% above the Bank of England base rate, plus a fixed compensation sum per invoice —
£40, £70 or £100 depending on the size of the debt. On top of the money you're owed.

You don't need it in your contract. It's statutory. It applies by default.

You can also claim reasonable costs of recovering the debt above that fixed sum.

Most firms never invoice it because they're frightened of losing the customer. Fair enough. But
knowing you can charge it changes the conversation, and putting it on the invoice as a line item —
"late payment interest may be applied under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act
1998" — costs you nothing and moves you up the payment run.

WHAT ACTUALLY GETS YOU PAID FASTER

It isn't chasing harder. It's four boring things:

1. Invoice the day the work is done, not at month end. Every day you sit on an invoice is a day added
   to your own payment terms, self-inflicted.
2. Invoice to a named person, not to "accounts". Accounts inboxes are where invoices go to wait.
3. Ask for the payment run date at the start of the job, not at the end. "When's your payment run?"
   asked in week one is a normal question. Asked in week nine it's a complaint.
4. Chase on a schedule, automatically, politely, forever. Day 1 after due, day 7, day 14, day 21,
   then a phone call. The firms that get paid aren't the loudest, they're the most consistent.

RETENTIONS
If you've got 3% or 5% held on jobs finished eighteen months ago, go and count it. Actually count it.
Most firms I onboard find between four and fifteen thousand pounds of retention they'd mentally
written off. Some of it is genuinely still claimable. The defects liability period ended; nobody
asked for it back.

Put every retention on a dated list with the release date. Diarise the release date. Ask on the day.

That's not clever accounting. That's just having a list. But nobody has the list.

#Construction #CashFlow #LatePayment #SmallBusinessUK

Quarter-end job 6 of 7: THE REVERSE CHARGE CHECK. Two minutes.

Open your last ten sales invoices. For each one, answer:

Was this construction work under CIS? Yes/No
Is the customer VAT registered? Yes/No
Is the customer CIS registered? Yes/No

## The four anchors that carry every year after this

Once you have been round once, the calendar writes itself. These repeat forever:

```
The 19th of every month     CIS return and payment
7 Aug / 7 Nov / 7 Feb / 7 May   MTD quarterly updates
31 January                  Final declaration, balancing payment, payment on account
5 April                     Tax year end — and each April, the next MTD threshold
```

Set a recurring reminder against each with three weeks' warning. Between them they generate roughly
half your content, they are never wrong, and your audience genuinely needs them.

## What to change, not just repeat

**Rewrite from real language.** By month 4 you will have had thirty-plus conversations. Everything in
this pack is written in my words, informed by research. It should be in theirs. Six discovery calls
in, start rewriting the anchors from what people actually said.

**Cut the bottom half.** By day 90 you will know which channels produce booked calls and which produce
nothing. Months 4–6 should run on fewer channels, harder — not the same spread out of habit.

**Promote what worked.** Whichever three posts got the most replies in months 1–3 should each become a
page on the site, an email, and a video. Most firms make something good once and never use it again.

**Add the case studies.** By month 4 you should have at least one client with three months of history
and something worth writing up. A real case study outperforms every generic post in this document.
