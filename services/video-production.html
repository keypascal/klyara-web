Ten-second phone clips of things you already look at: the before (walk the space slowly, that is all), progress moments (first fix done, plaster going on, the satisfying bits), the after (one slow pan of the finished job, one detail close-up), and incidental texture: the van arriving, the brew, the tidy-up: which sounds like nothing and edits into everything. The habit that works: film for thirty seconds at three moments per job: arrival, midpoint, done: and dump it all to us weekly, unsorted. No planning, no talking, no steady hands required. One kitchen refit filmed this way yields a before/after reel, a timelapse-style progress cut, two detail posts and a testimonial backdrop: five pieces of content from ninety seconds of filming. We send a one-page cheat sheet (and nag gently) until the habit sticks, which takes about three jobs. The firms that feed the machine best are never the media-savvy ones: they are the ones who bolted it to their existing routine.
