Fair questions,
straight answers.

It is honest if asked: the assistant never pretends to be human, and if a caller says 'am I speaking to a robot?' it says yes, cheerfully, and carries on helping. In practice almost nobody asks, because what callers actually notice is that the phone was answered on the second ring at 7pm by something polite that knew the trade. Compare that with the alternative they usually get: voicemail, or six rings and nothing. We have listened to thousands of these calls and the pattern is consistent: people care about being helped, not about the species of the helper. The transcripts land in your portal, so you can judge the tone yourself within the first week. And if a particular caller clearly wants a human, the assistant offers a callback and flags it urgent to your phone.
The assistant knows its limits, which is most of the skill. Anything outside its brief (a dispute, a supplier chasing money, an emergency on a live job, anything technical it has not been given) gets captured in detail and escalated: you get a text with the caller, number, summary and urgency, and the caller is told honestly when to expect a callback. It never bluffs an answer to get off the phone. You define the escalation rules during setup: some clients want everything above a simple enquiry pushed through immediately, others want only genuine emergencies interrupting a pour. The assistant also learns your patterns over the first weeks: the summaries get sharper as it sees which calls you treat as urgent. The worst case is exactly what a good human receptionist would do: take a proper message. The difference is this one never forgets to write it down.
Only the prices you have explicitly approved, and only in the way you have approved them. Most clients let it give day rates, call-out fees and rough ranges ('rewires typically start around X, but it depends on the property; shall I book a free survey?') because a caller with no number at all often rings the next firm. Anything beyond the approved list gets the honest answer: pricing depends on the job, and the right move is a survey, which it can book there and then. It will never improvise a figure, discount, or commit you to anything: the rules are hard limits, not suggestions. You can change the approved list any time from a quick message to us. In practice the ranges convert better than silence: people just want to know they are in the right postcode financially before giving up an evening to a quote visit.
Then answer: the assistant only picks up what you do not. The standard setup rings your mobile first for a few seconds (you choose how many) and the assistant takes over only when you are up a ladder, driving, or done for the day. Some clients flip it after a month and let the assistant take everything first, forwarding only genuine emergencies, once they have seen the quality of the summaries: that is your call and it is one setting. You can also set schedules: you take calls 8 to 4, the assistant owns evenings and weekends. Every call it takes, answered or missed by you first, produces the same transcript and summary in the portal. Nothing about the service asks you to stop being reachable; it just ends the era of the missed call costing you a job.
Rolling monthly, 30 days notice, like everything else we sell. No setup fee, no minimum term, no exit paperwork: if it stops earning its keep, you stop paying for it. We price it this way deliberately because the service sells itself in the transcripts: by week two you can count the enquiries that would previously have hit voicemail. If you pause it (quiet January, say), the number and setup are held so restarting is a switch, not a project. The only thing we ask is that you actually read the first fortnight of summaries: clients who do almost never leave, and clients who leave almost never did. The honest economics: one saved job a quarter typically pays for the year.
Live in two to three working days from the kickoff call. Day one we take the brief: your services, area, prices you are happy for it to quote, the questions you always get asked, and what counts as urgent. Day two we build and test it against your brief, calling it ourselves with the awkward questions your customers actually ask. Day three you call it, try to trip it up (please do), and we go live: either on a new number, or with your existing number forwarding when unanswered. Nothing changes about your phone or your habits. The brief keeps improving after launch: every time you correct a summary or add a price, the assistant gets sharper. Most clients are surprised that the slowest part is choosing the greeting.
