Enough to buy meaningful data, which locally usually means 300 to 500 pounds a month of ad spend to start: below that, clicks trickle in too slowly to learn anything, and you spend three months discovering what a proper month would have shown you. The arithmetic to hold onto: if a local trade click costs 2 to 6 pounds and one in ten quality visitors calls, 400 pounds buys roughly 80 to 150 clicks and perhaps 8 to 15 enquiries: now the only question is what an enquiry is worth to you, and for most trades winning even one 3,000-pound job a month makes the maths silly in your favour. If your realistic budget is under 300, we will say so on the call and point you at local SEO and Google Ready first, because thin ad budgets mostly buy education. Budgets scale up only when the numbers prove they should: we ask for more spend when cost-per-enquiry is beating your target, never to hit an agency growth quota. Every pound of spend is yours, in your account, visible.
Faster than anything else we sell: ads can put you at the top of the results the day the campaign switches on, and the first calls typically arrive inside the first week, sometimes the first afternoon. The honest curve: weeks one to four are calibration (finding which search terms, times and areas produce enquiries rather than tyre-kickers), so early cost-per-call runs high and then falls as the data compounds: judge the system at week six, not week one. Seasonality is real and we plan around it: emergency trades convert year-round, planned work (extensions, bathrooms) surges in spring and autumn. What ads cannot fix is what happens after the click: if calls go unanswered, the spend leaks: which is why campaigns pair naturally with the AI receptionist, and why we track booked jobs, not just calls. Immediate visibility is the product; the calibration month is the price of making it efficient.
No: you pay Google directly from your own ad account, we charge a flat management fee, and the two never mix: which matters more than it sounds.
