Getting paid

Payment terms that protect you: deposits, stages and interest

26 Jun 2026 · 10 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Terms on the quote, the order and the invoice beat terms mentioned once.
  • Deposits fund materials; stages tied to visible progress keep you cash positive.
  • Statutory interest and fixed recovery costs apply to B2B late payment automatically.
  • Stating the position calmly moves behaviour more than charging it ever does.

Chasing is triage. The real fight against late payment happens before the job starts, in the terms you set. Five clauses do most of the work.

1. A deposit that means commitment

On domestic work, a fair deposit filters the customers who were never going to pay well, and funds the materials you would otherwise finance yourself. Put the amount and what it covers in writing.

2. Stage payments tied to milestones

Never let the gap between money-in and work-done grow job-sized. Stages tied to visible milestones (out of the ground, wind and watertight, first fix) keep the customer invested and your exposure small. If a stage payment stalls, you stop; that clause has ended more disputes than any solicitor.

3. Payment days, in numbers

"Payment within 14 days of invoice" beats "prompt payment" every time. Vague terms are unenforceable; numbers are not.

4. Interest and compensation, quoted, not just threatened

Business customers: late payment legislation gives you statutory interest at 8% over base plus fixed compensation per invoice, whether or not your terms mention it. Mentioning it anyway, politely, on the quote, changes behaviour before behaviour needs changing.

5. Retention of title on materials

A line saying materials remain yours until paid for gives you standing in the worst-case conversation, especially around insolvency. Cheap clause, occasionally priceless.

Terms only work when the paperwork behind them is tidy: invoices out on time, statements accurate, reminders relentless. That second half is the part we automate.

Where terms need to appear

  1. 1
    On the quote

    Payment schedule, validity period, and what is excluded. This is where terms are actually agreed.

  2. 2
    On the acceptance

    A signature or written confirmation referencing the quote and its terms.

  3. 3
    On the invoice

    Due date in days, bank details, reference, and the statutory interest line.

  4. 4
    In the chase sequence

    Referenced calmly at day seven overdue, not invented at day thirty.

  5. 5
    In your head, consistently

    Terms you vary for whoever pushes hardest are not terms.

TermA sensible defaultWhy
Payment days14 days domestic, 30 commercialShorter is normal domestically and rarely questioned
Deposit10 to 25%Funds materials, secures the slot

Final balance5 to 10%Small enough that a snag cannot hold it hostage

Defaults, not rules. The point is having them written down before the conversation, not inventing them during it.

The sentence that does the work

'Payment is due within 14 days. Statutory interest and recovery costs apply to late payment.' On every invoice, every time. Firms report faster payment from printing that line without ever charging a penny of interest.

Terms are not a threat. They are the difference between a business and a favour.
