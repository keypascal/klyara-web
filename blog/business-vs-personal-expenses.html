MTD and tax

Business or personal? The expenses line builders keep crossing

6 Apr 2026 · 11 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • The test is wholly and exclusively for the business. Mixed use gets apportioned.
  • Protective clothing and branded workwear qualify; ordinary clothes never do, even if only worn for work.
  • Everyday lunch on your usual patch is not claimable. Genuine travel away from your normal pattern often is.
  • Firms lose far more to under-claiming than over-claiming. Capture the evidence in the moment.

The expenses line is where honest people get nervous, mostly because the rules sound stricter than they are and vaguer than they should be. The test is simple to state: an expense is allowable if it is incurred wholly and exclusively for the business. The difficulty is that real life is rarely wholly and exclusively anything.

Apportionment done properly

Pick a percentage you can explain, apply it consistently, and write down how you arrived at it once. 'Sixty percent business on the phone because that is roughly the split of use' is defensible. Ninety percent with no reasoning is an invitation.

The cost of being too cautious

Firms lose more to under-claiming than to over-claiming. Fuel receipts not captured, tools bought in cash, the home office never claimed, subsistence on genuinely distant jobs written off as too much hassle. Every one of those is relief you were entitled to and gave away. The answer is not aggression; it is evidence, captured in the moment.

The app codes each receipt as it arrives and a specialist reviews the borderline ones monthly, which is where most of this stops being a worry. Ask about your own grey areas.

The three tests, applied to real situations

Ask three questions of any spend and the answer is usually obvious: would you have incurred this if you did not run the business? Is there a private benefit? Can you evidence and apportion it defensibly?

Use of home: two roads

The flat rate is simple and needs no bills. The proportional method can be worth more if you genuinely use a dedicated space, but it requires household costs, a floor-area calculation and a use-time apportionment. Most trades are better off with the flat rate and the time saved.

Limited company owners: a different set of rules

If you trade through a company, the money is the company's, not yours, and drawings are not expenses. Personal spend on the company card becomes a director's loan or a benefit in kind, both of which have tax consequences and both of which are wholly avoidable with a separate card and a little discipline. The single cheapest habit for a company director is never putting personal spend on the business account in the first place.

The monthly ten minutes that keeps this clean

  • Every receipt captured on the day, coded to a job where relevant
  • Anything genuinely mixed flagged for review rather than guessed
  • One consistent apportionment percentage per category, written down once
  • Personal spend kept off business accounts entirely
  • Borderline items queried with your bookkeeper the same month, not at year end
