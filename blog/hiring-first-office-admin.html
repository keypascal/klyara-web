Running the firm

When to hire office help, and what it actually costs

18 May 2026 · 11 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • The first hire that pays for itself is usually admin, not another pair of hands on the tools.
  • Hand over the phone, the chasing and quote follow-ups first. Keep bookkeeping with a specialist.
  • True employment cost runs 20 to 25 percent above gross pay once NI, pension and holiday are counted.
  • Log a week of admin tasks before hiring. Half are usually automatable, and the rest write the job description.

There is a moment in most growing firms when the owner is doing eleven hours on site and three more on paperwork, and something has to give. The instinct is to hire another pair of hands on the tools. Often the better first hire is the one that gives you your evenings back.

Typical trigger10+ hrsa week of admin, or quotes going out late
Part-time admin£13-16per hour in most regions, 10 to 15 hours a week
True cost+20-25%on top of gross, once NI, pension and holiday are counted

The three jobs to hand over first

  1. 1
    Answering the phone

    Every missed call is a lost quote. This is the highest-value hour of admin in any trade business, and the easiest to delegate to a person or a system.

  2. 2
    Chasing money

    Invoices chased on a schedule get paid weeks earlier. Nobody enjoys it, which is exactly why it should not depend on your mood on a Friday.

  3. 3
    Quote follow-ups

    The work is already half won. A polite check-in at day three and day ten converts jobs that would otherwise drift to a competitor.

Notice that none of those three is bookkeeping. Bookkeeping is the one piece of admin where a part-timer without training costs you more than it saves, because errors in CIS and VAT surface months later as penalties and rework. Outsource the discipline, delegate the diary.

Before you hire anyone

Write down every admin task you did last week and how long it took. Half of them usually turn out to be automatable, and the remaining half define the job description exactly.

Plenty of firms find the honest answer is a phone system and a bookkeeper rather than a salary. Compare what each option costs.

The Employment Allowance

Most small employers can reduce their employer's National Insurance bill by a meaningful annual allowance. It is claimed through payroll and it is routinely missed by firms running payroll themselves. Worth checking your eligibility before you conclude a hire is unaffordable.

Employee, subcontractor, or neither

Before hiring, decide honestly which of three things you actually need. An employee gives you continuity, control and loyalty, at the cost of NI, pension, holiday and employment rights. A subcontractor gives you flexibility, but only if the arrangement is genuinely self-employed in substance, not just in paperwork. An outsourced service gives you a specific outcome with no employment risk at all, which is why bookkeeping, payroll and call answering are usually bought rather than hired at this size.

Hire when

  • The work is continuous and predictable
  • You need someone under your direction
  • The role grows into more responsibility
  • You can fund three quiet months

Outsource when

  • The work is specialist and periodic
  • Mistakes carry regulatory risk
  • Volume is uneven
  • You want it to run without managing anyone
  1. 1
    Write the job down before you advertise

    Tasks, hours, and what good looks like after ninety days.

  2. 2
    Check right to work before day one

    Not after. It is a legal requirement with real penalties.

  3. 3
    Register as an employer and set up payroll

    This takes days, not minutes, so start it before the start date.

  4. 4
    Enrol them in a pension from the first eligible payday

    Auto-enrolment duties begin immediately; postponement is a choice you make deliberately.

  5. 5

    New starters who know what success looks like become useful twice as fast.
