What should bookkeeping cost a small building firm?

6 July 2026, 10 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • DIY is not free: value your own evenings at your day rate and it is the most expensive option.
  • Construction-specialist bookkeeping runs roughly £99 to £329 a month for a small firm.
  • Cheap becomes expensive when CIS, the reverse charge or MTD deadlines are handled wrongly.
  • Onboarding fees are normal: they cover cleaning up the backlog before the monthly rhythm starts.

Ask what bookkeeping should cost and you will get quotes from £25 a month to £800, often for what sounds like the same thing. It is not the same thing. Here is the honest map of the UK market for a small construction firm in 2026, including where we sit and why.

Under 50 a month: software plus you

At this level you are buying software, not a service. You do the work: snap receipts, categorise transactions, chase your own mistakes, file your own returns. For a brand-new sole trader with a dozen transactions a month it can be fine. The hidden cost is your evenings, and the expensive part is what you do not know: the reverse charge coded wrong, materials not split, a CIS statement missing. Software does not tap you on the shoulder.

50 to 150: the generalist bookkeeper

A real human, usually good, usually serving cafes, consultants and one or two builders. The construction-specific work, CIS returns, subbie verification, reverse charge, retentions, sits at the edge of their world instead of the centre. Things are mostly right, and mostly is where penalties live. This tier also hides the classic capacity trap: one person, no cover, and your books stop when their life happens.

150 to 350: the specialist, done properly

This is where a construction firm with VAT, CIS and a monthly rhythm should expect to sit, and it is where Klyara Trade at 199 and Pro at 329 live. What the money buys: a specialist who does construction all day, software included in the price, CIS and reverse charge handled as the default rather than the exception, monthly figures you can act on, deadlines tracked on a board rather than in someone's head, and written continuity so the service does not depend on one person's diary. At this level bookkeeping stops being a cost and starts being the thing that catches the £2,000 mistakes.

350 and up: the practice with a payroll of its own

Bigger firms, management accounts, forecasting, a finance function in all but name. Worth it when you have the turnover to feed it. A 10-person building firm usually does not need this yet, and paying for it early buys polish, not profit.

The four questions that expose a cheap quote

  • Who files my CIS return, and what happens if the 19th is missed? Vague answer, walk away.
  • Show me a reverse charge invoice you have coded. A specialist smiles; a generalist blinks.
  • Is the software included, and who owns my data if I leave? Both answers should be yes and you.
  • What happens when you are on holiday? The only good answer is a named arrangement, in writing.

The questions worth asking any bookkeeper

Ask these five before you sign anything

  • Are you supervised for anti-money-laundering, and by whom? It is a legal requirement, and it is checkable
  • How many construction clients do you handle, and do you file CIS returns yourself?
  • How do you handle the VAT domestic reverse charge on my invoices?
  • Are you an HMRC-registered agent able to file MTD updates for me?
  • What exactly is in the monthly fee, and what is billed separately?

Why onboarding fees are normal

Almost every new client arrives with a backlog. A one-off catch-up fee covers rebuilding and reconciling the past so the monthly fee only ever pays for the future. A firm that quotes a monthly price with no setup fee is either absorbing that cost elsewhere or is about to discover your backlog.
