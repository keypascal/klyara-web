Five reasons builders get paid late, and the fix for each one

13 July 2026, 11 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Late payment is a process problem, not a people problem, in four cases out of five.
  • Terms stated on quote, order and invoice beat terms mentioned once in conversation.
  • Automatic chasing from day one overdue moves payment weeks earlier.
  • Statutory interest and fixed recovery costs apply to B2B invoices automatically.

Late payment in construction is not weather. It does not just happen to you. It has causes, the causes repeat, and most of them are fixable from your side of the invoice. Here are the five we see most across small firms' books, with the fix for each.

The invoice arrives late, so it pays late

The job finished Friday; the invoice went out the following Thursday, missed that week's payment run, and joined the back of next month's queue. Payment terms start when the invoice lands, not when the work did.

The fix: invoice the day the job completes, from your phone, before you leave site. Firms on Klyara raise invoices in the portal or app in under a minute, and the clock starts immediately.

The invoice is easy to dispute

Vague descriptions, missing order numbers, labour and materials lumped together, the wrong VAT treatment. Every ambiguity is a legitimate-sounding reason to park your invoice in the query pile, and query piles do not pay.

The fix: boring precision. Reference their order number, describe the work as they would, split labour and materials, and get the reverse charge wording exactly right. An invoice with nothing to query is an invoice that enters the payment run.

Nobody chases, or the wrong person does

You priced the work, did the work, and now you are also the debt collector, ringing the same contact you need for the next contract. So you soften it, delay it, and let it slide. They know you will.

The fix: depersonalise it. Chasing from your bookkeeping team on a schedule, polite then firmer then a call then a late-fee letter, reads as a firm with systems, not a mate asking a favour. It is why chasing is built into Trade and Pro rather than sold as an optional extra.

The terms invited it

Thirty-day terms on a labour-heavy job means you bankroll their project for a month, and habitual late payers stretch whatever you write anyway because there has never been a consequence.

The fix: shorter terms where you can get them, deposits or stage payments on bigger jobs, and statutory late-payment interest wording on the invoice. UK law backs interest plus compensation on late commercial invoices; often the mention alone gets the invoice paid.

You cannot see it building up

When the books run months behind, you do not notice a customer quietly stretching from 30 days to 45 to 60 until the hole is four invoices deep. By then you have priced two more jobs for them.

The fix: live books. A debtors list you can read on your phone shows the drift at invoice two, not invoice six, while it is still a conversation rather than a write-off. That visibility is the quiet reason good bookkeeping pays for itself.

The chase sequence that works

  • Day 0
    Invoice, same day

    Sent the day the work or stage completes, with terms visible on the document.

  • Day 7
    Friendly check

    Confirming it arrived and asking whether anything is needed to process it. Catches lost invoices and missing PO numbers.

  • Due date
    Polite reminder

    Sent on the day, automatically, before it is even late.

  • Day 7 overdue
    Firm and specific

    Reference the terms, state the statutory position calmly, ask for a payment date.

  • Day 14
    Phone call

    Not email. The call is what changes behaviour.

  • Day 30
    Formal notice

    Interest and recovery costs applied, final request before escalation.

Six touches, all polite, most automatic. Firms that run this get paid weeks earlier than firms that chase when they remember.

The invoice detail that gets you paid faster

Purchase order number, correct contact, correct email, a clear payment reference and bank details on the face of the invoice. A surprising share of late payments are simply invoices that never reached the person who authorises them.

Firms that get paid on time

  • Terms on quote, order and invoice
  • Same-day invoicing
  • Automatic chase sequence
  • Deposits and stages on bigger jobs

Firms that do not

  • Terms mentioned once, verbally
  • Invoices sent in a Sunday batch
  • Chasing when cash gets tight
  • Full credit, always, to everyone
