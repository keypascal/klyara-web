MTD and tax

Van, tools, fuel: what a tradesperson can actually claim

5 Aug 2026 · 11 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Vans and tools are allowable; ordinary clothes and everyday lunches are not.
  • Mileage at 45p or actual running costs: choose deliberately, because it sticks per vehicle.
  • Phone and home use get apportioned honestly, with the reasoning written down once.
  • Under-claiming costs the trade far more than over-claiming. Capture the evidence at purchase.

Every allowable expense you fail to record is tax you volunteered to pay. Here is what a tradesperson can genuinely claim, minus the van-myths from the group chat.

The van

Two routes. Simplified mileage: a flat rate per business mile (45p for the first 10,000, 25p after) covering fuel, insurance, repairs, the lot. Actual costs: record everything the van really costs, claim the business share, and claim capital allowances on the purchase. Heavy-mileage trades with an expensive van usually win on actual costs; light users win on mileage. Pick once, keep records either way.

Tools and equipment

Hand tools, power tools, ladders, mixers: allowable. Bigger kit typically goes through capital allowances, and the Annual Investment Allowance means most trades get full relief in year one anyway. The only thing that kills these claims is missing receipts, and tool receipts die in van doors at a legendary rate.

The rest of the honest list

Materials for jobs. PPE and genuine workwear (branded or protective, not nice jeans). Phone and broadband, business share. Public liability insurance. Trade subscriptions and certification renewals. Accountancy and bookkeeping fees, pleasingly. Parking for jobs, yes; parking fines, never, and no, not even that one.

The habit that makes it all real

None of this survives an HMRC question without evidence. The fix is the ten-second snap: photograph every receipt the moment it exists and let the software file it. Under Making Tax Digital that habit stops being good practice and becomes the law anyway. Get ahead of it here.

The cash-purchase problem

Saturday morning at the merchant, paid from your pocket, no receipt kept. It is the single largest source of lost relief in the trade and it is fixed by one habit: ask for the receipt, photograph it at the counter, walk away.
