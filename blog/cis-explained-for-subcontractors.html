CIS, from the subbie's side

CIS explained for subcontractors: deductions, statements, refunds and gross status

20 July 2026, 13 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • The 20% is your own income tax and NI, paid in advance on the labour part of your invoice.
  • Unregistered means 30%. Registration is free and immediate.
  • Deduction statements are your refund evidence. Chase missing ones monthly, not in January.
  • Gross payment status removes deductions entirely once you pass three tests.

The Construction Industry Scheme has run since before most of us were on the tools, and it still gets explained worse than any tax rule in Britain. Here it is, honestly, from the subbie's side of the fence.

What CIS actually is

CIS is HMRC's way of collecting tax from construction up front. When a contractor pays a subcontractor for construction work, they must deduct a slice of the labour amount and send it to HMRC as an advance on the subbie's tax. It is not an extra tax. It is your own income tax and National Insurance, paid early, before you ever see it.

The three rates, and why yours matters

  • 20%: the standard rate for subbies registered with CIS. Most of the trade sits here.
  • 30%: the punishment rate for subbies who are not registered, or whose details do not match HMRC's records. If you are on 30 and did not choose to be, fixing your registration is free money.
  • Gross payment status, zero percent: HMRC trusts you to pay your own tax later. You need a clean compliance history and turnover above the thresholds. Brilliant for cash flow, and it makes your quarterly figures the early-warning system for the bill building up.

Deductions come off labour, not materials

Contractors deduct CIS from the labour element only. Invoice materials separately and clearly, and that money arrives untouched. Lump everything into one line and you invite over-deduction: an interest-free loan to HMRC until refund time.

This is the cheapest habit in construction: split labour and materials on every invoice, every time.

The deduction statement: your proof

Every month a contractor deducts from you, they must give you a statement showing what was paid and what was held back. Those statements are your evidence at year end. Lost statements are the number one cause of delayed CIS refunds, so chase them monthly, not in a January panic. In the Klyara portal they are filed the day they arrive, automatically.

How refunds really work

Because 20% is deducted from gross labour with no allowance for your expenses or personal allowance, most subbies overpay across the year. The overpayment comes back after your return or final declaration is filed. Two things speed it up: clean records that reconcile to the penny, and filing early. Two things slow it to a crawl: missing statements and figures that do not add up. This is why subbies with tidy books get refunds in weeks while their mates wait months.

When you become the contractor

Take on your first subbie and you cross the fence: now you must register as a contractor, verify each subbie with HMRC before first payment, deduct at the rate HMRC gives you, file a CIS return by the 19th of every month, and issue statements. Miss a monthly return and penalties start at £100 and climb. This is the exact moment most one-person firms move to Klyara Trade or Pro and stop thinking about the 19th forever.

Where the deduction actually goes

The money is not lost, and it is not a fee. Your contractor sends it to HMRC against your name and it sits there as tax already paid. At year end, your actual liability is calculated on your profit, the deductions are set against it, and the difference goes one way or the other. For most full-time subbies with real expenses, the difference is a refund.

The statement discipline

Do these and refunds stop being painful

  • Collect a deduction statement from every contractor, every month you were paid
  • Store them by tax month, not in a pile: reconstructing twelve of them in January is how refunds die
  • Check the figures match what actually landed in your bank
  • Chase missing ones the same month, politely and immediately
  • Register for CIS if you have not: 30% instead of 20 is a self-inflicted cash-flow wound

CIS is not an extra tax. It is your own tax, collected early, by someone else, on the labour half of your invoice.
