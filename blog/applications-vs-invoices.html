Getting paid

Applications for payment vs invoices: get the paperwork right

17 Jul 2026 · 10 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • On commercial contracts you apply for payment; the invoice follows certification.
  • Miss the application deadline and you wait a full cycle, however good the work was.
  • Payment notices and pay-less notices have statutory deadlines that protect you.
  • VAT timing follows the certified amount, not the applied-for amount.

On commercial and subcontract work, the paperwork has a sequence: you send an application for payment, the payer responds with a payment notice, and only then does an invoice usually enter the picture. Firms that treat an application like an invoice, or vice versa, pay for the confusion in delayed cash and messy VAT.

What each document is

An application is your claim for the value of work done this period: measured, itemised, sent by the date the contract sets. It is not yet a tax document. The payment notice (or the contract's default) establishes the sum due. The invoice then bills that agreed sum, and under the reverse charge it is the invoice that carries the VAT treatment.

The two classic mistakes

Invoicing instead of applying. Miss the contract's application window and on many contracts you wait a whole cycle to claim, thirty days of cash flow lost to a date. The application deadline is a payday deadline; treat it like one.

Accounting for VAT off applications. Applications are not VAT invoices. Book VAT from them and your return is wrong in one direction or the other, which is exactly the kind of pattern HMRC's systems now spot automatically.

The rhythm that gets you paid

Application in on the contract date, every cycle, no exceptions. Notice checked against your claim, queried fast when short. Invoice raised to match, coded correctly for the reverse charge. It is administration, which is why it slips on busy months, and why ours never does. Books that speak fluent construction.

The notice sequence that protects you

  • Application date
    You apply

    On the date set by the contract, for work done to a valuation date. Miss it and you wait a full cycle.

  • Due date
    The payment becomes due

    Fixed by the contract, usually a set number of days after the application.

  • Payment notice
    They tell you the sum

    Within five days of the due date, stating what they consider due and how it was calculated.

  • Pay-less notice
    Or they reduce it

    Must be issued before the final date for payment, with the basis of the reduction.

  • Final date for payment
    Money moves

    If no valid payment or pay-less notice was issued, the notified sum is payable in full.

The notice regime exists to protect you. Firms that know these dates get paid; firms that do not accept whatever arrives.

The 'smash and grab' principle, plainly

If the paying party fails to issue a valid payment or pay-less notice in time, the amount you applied for generally becomes the sum due. That is not a loophole; it is the statutory consequence of the notice regime. It is also why your application needs to be properly detailed and on time.

Applying is not invoicing. VAT timing follows the certified sum, which is why mixing the two creates VAT errors as well as cash-flow ones.

Getting paid on commercial work

  • Know your application dates and diarise them for the whole contract at the start
  • Submit detailed applications with measured quantities, not round numbers
  • Record when notices arrive, and check they are valid and in time
  • Invoice promptly once certified, for the certified amount
  • Track retention separately from the main balance
