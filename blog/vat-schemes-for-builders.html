VAT schemes for builders: standard, cash accounting or flat rate?

12 Aug 2026 · 11 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Standard, cash accounting and flat rate are three ways to run the same tax with different cash flow.
  • Cash accounting means you pay VAT when paid, which suits firms with slow customers.
  • The reverse charge broke the flat rate for most of the trade: reverse-charge sales carry no output VAT.
  • Run your own numbers before choosing; the right answer changed in 2021 for most subbies.

Two builders with identical turnover can have completely different VAT cash flow, purely because of which scheme they sit on. Nobody chooses these on purpose; they inherit whatever got ticked at registration. Worth ten minutes to check yours.

Standard accounting

VAT is due on invoices when you raise them, reclaimed on purchases when you are invoiced. Clean, universal, and dangerous in construction: raise a 20,000 pound invoice in March, get paid in June, and the VAT is due in between, out of money you have not received.

Cash accounting

Available to most firms under 1.35 million turnover: VAT is due when you are paid, not when you invoice. For a trade where 60-day terms are a compliment, this single switch can be the difference between a comfortable quarter and an overdraft conversation. If your customers pay slowly and you are on standard accounting, this is usually the first thing to fix.

Flat rate, and why the reverse charge broke it

The flat rate scheme pays a fixed percentage of gross turnover and skips reclaiming input VAT, which once suited simple labour-only firms. Then the domestic reverse charge arrived: most B2B construction income now carries no output VAT, but flat rate users still cannot reclaim inputs properly. For the majority of CIS firms the sums stopped working in 2021, and plenty are still on it out of habit, quietly overpaying.

Which one is yours?

It is printed inside your VAT registration, or ask your bookkeeper, or ask us and we will tell you in one look at your account. Scheme reviews are part of onboarding on every VAT-inclusive plan, because it is the cheapest win in the whole setup.

The three schemes, side by side

SchemeHow it worksSuits
StandardCharge output VAT, reclaim input VAT, pay the difference quarterlyMost firms, especially with high materials spend
Cash accountingVAT accounted when money moves, not when invoicedFirms with slow-paying customers or bad-debt exposure
Flat ratePay a fixed percentage of gross turnover, limited input reclaimService-heavy firms with low materials spend, rarely now
Annual accountingOne return a year with instalmentsFirms wanting predictable admin, not usually a cash win

Eligibility limits apply to cash accounting, flat rate and annual accounting. Check thresholds before assuming you qualify.

Why the flat rate stopped working for the trade

Reverse-charge sales carry no output VAT, but the flat rate is a percentage of gross turnover. Firms invoicing mostly business-to-business found themselves paying a percentage on income that carried no VAT at all, while losing input reclaim on materials. For most subbies the flat rate went from a small win to a straightforward loss overnight.

Choosing, on your own numbers

  1. 1
    Total last year's sales, split by reverse charge and normal VAT

    This single split usually decides the answer.

  2. 2
    Total the input VAT you reclaimed on materials, fuel and plant

    Heavy materials spend argues strongly for standard.

  3. 3
    Check how long customers actually take to pay

    Slow payers argue for cash accounting: you pay VAT when paid, not when invoiced.

  4. 4
    Model each scheme against those figures

    One afternoon, once a year. Schemes can be changed, within the rules.

  5. 5
    If you are consistently in repayment, switch to monthly returns

    Not a scheme change, just a frequency change, and it accelerates every refund.
