CIS

Taking on your first subbie: the compliance checklist

24 Jun 2026 · 11 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Paying another tradesperson for construction work makes you a contractor in HMRC's eyes.
  • Register as a contractor, verify each subbie before first payment, deduct at HMRC's rate.
  • File a monthly return by the 19th, including nil returns, and issue deduction statements.
  • Check employment status too: a subbie who looks like staff may legally be staff.

The first time you pay another tradesperson to help on a job, something bigger happens than an extra name on the WhatsApp group. In HMRC's eyes you have become a contractor, and a whole rulebook switches on that day, not at year end.

The checklist

1. Register as a contractor. Before the first payment, you register for CIS as a contractor with HMRC, separate from your existing registration as a subbie.

2. Verify every subbie. Also before first payment, verify each subcontractor with HMRC. Verification tells you the deduction rate: 20 percent for the registered, 30 for the unregistered, zero for gross status holders. Skip verification and deduct the wrong amount, and the shortfall becomes your problem.

3. Check they are genuinely self-employed. If you control their hours, provide all the tools and they carry no risk, HMRC may see an employee, and the PAYE bill lands on you retrospectively. Get the status honest from the start.

4. File monthly, forever. A CIS return is due by the 19th of every month, including months you paid nobody, until you tell HMRC the scheme is dormant. Nil returns are the ones firms forget, and penalties start at 100 pounds a time.

5. Issue deduction statements. Every subbie you deducted from gets a written statement each month. It is their proof for their refund; you would want the same courtesy.

The honest summary

None of this is hard. All of it is relentless, twelve times a year, on top of a business to run. This exact package is why Trade and Pro exist: verification, deductions, returns and statements all handled, from 199 pounds a month.

The full contractor checklist

  1. 1
    Register as a contractor with HMRC

    Separate from registering as a subcontractor. Do it before the first payment, not after.

  2. 2
    Verify each subbie before paying them

    HMRC returns a verification number and a rate: 20, 30 or gross. Record both.

  3. 3
    Deduct at the rate HMRC gave you

    From labour only, with materials properly itemised and excluded.

  4. 4
    File a monthly return by the 19th

    Every month, including nil returns for months where you paid nobody.

  5. 5
    Pay deductions over by the 22nd

    Electronically. The 19th if paying by post.

  6. 6
    Issue deduction statements within 14 days

    Every subbie, every month they were paid. It is their refund evidence and a legal duty.

  7. 7
    Check employment status honestly

    A 'subbie' who works only for you, with your tools, under your direction, may legally be an employee.

The whole job takes minutes a month when it runs to a checklist, and costs four figures when it does not.

Nil returns are the number-one penalty

A quiet month with no subbie payments still needs a return. The penalty for forgetting is exactly the same 100 pounds as any other late filing, and it is the most common automatic penalty in construction.
