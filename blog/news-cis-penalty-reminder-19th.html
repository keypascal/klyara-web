Today is the 19th: the CIS return deadline, and the penalty ladder behind it

19 Aug 2026 · 9 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • CIS returns for the tax month ending 5 August are due with HMRC today, the 19th.
  • A return one day late is an automatic £100 penalty, climbing to £300 or more as months pass.
  • Nil returns count: paid no subbies this month? A nil return is still due.
  • Payment of deductions is due by the 22nd if paying electronically.

A standing note published on the day it matters: if you paid subcontractors during the tax month that ended on the 5th, your CIS return is due with HMRC today. This is the deadline that generates more automatic penalties in construction than any other, almost entirely from two avoidable causes.

The penalty ladder, exactly

How lateWhat it costs
1 day£100, automatically
2 monthsa further £200
6 months£300 more, or 5% of the deductions if higher
12 monthsanother £300 or 5%, and potentially much more if HMRC treats the failure as deliberate

Per return. Miss three months in a row and the fixed penalties alone approach four figures before anyone at HMRC has looked at your case.

The two ways firms get caught

The nil-return trap

  • A quiet month with no subbie payments
  • 'No return needed, surely'
  • The £100 penalty arrives anyway
  • Fix: file the nil return, two minutes online

The verification drift

  • A new subbie starts mid-month
  • Paid at 20% on their say-so
  • HMRC's rate was 30%, and the gap is your debt
  • Fix: verify before the first payment, every time

If the 19th has already gone

File today anyway. The penalty for one day late and one month late is the same £100, but the six-month escalations are where real money goes, and a pattern of lateness is what threatens gross payment status. Then appeal if you have a reasonable excuse: HMRC does accept them.

On Klyara Trade and Pro this deadline simply stops existing as a thing you think about: verification, deductions, statements and the return run to a checklist, and you approve from your phone. See the monthly CIS run.

What has to be on the return

Every monthly CIS return declares

  • Every subcontractor paid in the tax month, with their verification details
  • Gross amount paid, excluding VAT
  • Cost of materials, which is excluded from deduction
  • The amount deducted from each subcontractor
  • A declaration that employment status has been considered for each one
  • A declaration that every subcontractor has been verified

The employment status declaration

Two of those lines are declarations, not data. You are formally confirming you have considered whether each person is genuinely self-employed. It is easy to tick and consequential to be wrong about, which is why status reviews belong in your annual routine.

If you have missed one

  1. 1
    File it today anyway

    One day late and one month late both cost £100. The escalations at two, six and twelve months are where real money is.

  2. 2
    Pay what is owed

    Deductions are due by the 22nd if paying electronically. Late payment carries interest separately from filing penalties.

  3. 3
    Appeal if you have a reasonable excuse

    HMRC does accept genuine ones: serious illness, bereavement, HMRC system failure. File first, then appeal.

  4. 4
    Fix the process, not just the month

    Missed returns are almost always a system failure rather than an intention. A checklist and a calendar entry solve it permanently.

  5. 5
    Check gross payment status exposure

    If you hold gross status, a pattern of late returns is what threatens it at the annual review.

The nil return is the most expensive two minutes in construction: skipping it costs exactly the same hundred pounds as skipping a real one.
