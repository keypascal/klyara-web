Job costing: knowing which work makes money and which quietly does not

25 May 2026 · 12 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Firm-level profit is an average that hides your loss-making work.
  • Give every job a code and allocate costs as they arrive, not at month end.
  • Include your own labour at a real rate, or the job is subsidised rather than profitable.
  • Most firms respond by dropping one job type, not by raising all prices.

Firm-level profit is an average, and averages hide the two jobs that lost money and the one that carried the quarter. Job costing is simply knowing which was which, and it changes what you quote for far more than any pricing theory.

Time to runMinutesa week, if the coding happens as you go
What it revealsThe losersusually one job type, consistently
Typical finding10-20%margin variance between job types nobody had measured

What it takes

  1. 1
    Give every job a code

    A short reference on every quote, invoice, receipt and timesheet. That is the entire administrative burden.

  2. 2
    Code costs as they arrive

    Materials to the job, subbie invoices to the job, hire to the job. Done at capture, it costs nothing extra.

  3. 3
    Include your own labour at a real rate

    A job that only profits because you worked unpaid Saturdays is not profitable; it is subsidised.

  4. 4
    Compare quoted against actual, every time

    Ten minutes at the end of each job. The pattern appears after about five jobs and rarely surprises anyone twice.

What firms do with the answer

Rarely raise prices across the board. Usually they stop quoting for one job type, adjust their contingency on another, and discover the work they enjoyed least was also the work paying least.

The bit that makes it stick

Job costing dies when it depends on someone remembering to allocate things at month end. It survives when coding happens at the moment of capture: the receipt photographed on site is tagged to the job there and then, the subbie invoice is coded when it is entered, the hire is coded on the order. Then the report is a by-product rather than a project.

We set job coding up as standard on Trade and Pro, so the margin-by-job report arrives with your monthly pack without anyone chasing it. See what is included.

Setting it up in a week

  1. 1
    Number your jobs

    A simple sequence: 26-014. Put it on the quote, and it flows to every invoice, receipt and timesheet after that.

  2. 2
    Tag costs at capture

    Photograph the receipt, pick the job. Two seconds. Doing it later is what kills job costing in most firms.

  3. 3
    Log labour honestly

    Including your own, at a real rate. A job that only profits because you worked free Saturdays is not profitable.

  4. 4
    Close the job when it finishes

    Quoted against actual, in ten minutes, while you still remember what happened.

  5. 5
    Review the pattern quarterly

    Five jobs give you a hint. Twenty give you a strategy.

What you learnWhat firms typically do about it
One job type consistently losesStop quoting for it, or reprice it properly
Materials wastage on a specific tradeChange supplier, order differently, or charge handling
Labour overruns on a job sizeAdjust the estimating multiplier for that size
A customer type always overrunsPrice a contingency in for them, or decline politely
Small jobs eat marginIntroduce a minimum charge and a call-out rate

Every one of these is invisible at firm level and obvious at job level.

The insurance work discovery

Firms costing jobs properly frequently discover that insurance and warranty work, which felt like guaranteed money, carries the thinnest margin of anything they do once the admin time is counted. It is one of the most common findings, and one of the most profitable to act on.

The final margin is nine percent, and the biggest single loss is work that was done and never charged for.

The variation discipline

Unbilled variations are the largest single leak in fixed-price work, and they are almost never dishonesty by the customer. They are a builder saying "yes, no problem" on a Tuesday and forgetting by Friday. Job costing surfaces them because the cost lands against the job while the income does not, and the gap is visible within a week rather than at year end.

The habits that hold margin

  • Every job has a number, used on every document
  • Variations priced in writing before the work, always
  • Own labour logged at a real rate
  • Quoted vs actual reviewed at the end of every job
  • Quarterly review of margin by job type, and the courage to drop the worst one
