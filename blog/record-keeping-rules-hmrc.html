MTD and tax

What records must you keep, and for how long?

20 Apr 2026 · 10 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Keep records six years from the end of the tax year they relate to. Longer if an enquiry is open.
  • Under MTD the records must be digital from the start, not retyped at the deadline.
  • A clear photo of a receipt is a valid record. The faded paper original is not required once captured.
  • One business bank account and same-day receipt capture survive an enquiry with no drama.

Nobody thinks about record keeping until an HMRC letter arrives, and by then the answer is already decided by what you kept. The rules are simpler than the anxiety around them, and under Making Tax Digital they now come with a format requirement as well as a time limit.

Digital copies are fine. A clear photograph of a receipt is a valid record; the faded paper original is not required once captured.

The six-year rule, precisely

Keep records for at least six years from the end of the tax year concerned, and longer where an enquiry is open or an asset is still owned. Limited companies keep company records for six years from the end of the accounting period. Where you have claimed capital allowances on a van or plant, keep the purchase paperwork for as long as you own it, plus the six years after disposal.

The MTD change people miss

Digital record keeping is not the same as having a digital copy. The requirement is that your records live in software from the start and the quarterly updates come from that software, rather than being retyped into a portal at the deadline.

The habits that survive an enquiry

  • Photograph every receipt the moment it exists, not at the weekend
  • One business bank account, used only for business
  • Never pay cash without capturing the evidence at the counter
  • Keep deduction statements filed by month, not in a drawer
  • Reconcile monthly so gaps surface within weeks, not years

Every Klyara plan captures receipts through the app and stores them against the entry they support, so an enquiry becomes a search rather than an archaeology project. See the monthly rhythm.

What an enquiry actually feels like

Most HMRC enquiries into small trade businesses are not dramatic. A letter arrives asking about a specific area: your motor expenses, a materials figure, a year where turnover jumped. You are given a deadline to produce records. Firms with clean digital records answer in an afternoon and the matter closes. Firms without spend weeks reconstructing, and reconstruction itself invites more questions, because gaps look like evasion even when they are only untidiness.

  • Opening letter
    A specific question

    Usually one area, with a deadline of around thirty days

  • Your response
    Records and explanation

    Complete, organised, and on time closes most enquiries here

  • Follow-up
    Clarification

    Where figures do not reconcile, or evidence is missing

  • Outcome
    Closure or adjustment

    Adjustments carry interest, and penalties depend on behaviour

  • Behaviour matters
    Careless vs deliberate

    Cooperation and good records reduce penalties substantially

Good records do not just prove the numbers. They evidence that you took reasonable care, which directly reduces penalties.

The digital link rule

Under MTD, the chain from your records to the submission must be digital. Copying a figure from software into a portal by hand breaks that link. It sounds pedantic; it is exactly what the legislation requires, and it is why we file from the same records the books are kept in.

Records that answer in an afternoon

  • Receipts photographed on the day
  • One business bank account
  • Everything reconciled monthly
  • Job references on every document

Records that cost weeks

  • A carrier bag and a memory
  • Personal and business money mixed
  • Reconciled once a year, if that
  • Cash purchases with no evidence
