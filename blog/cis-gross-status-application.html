Applying for gross payment status: the three tests, step by step

3 Aug 2026 · 10 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Gross status means contractors pay you in full, with no CIS deduction.
  • Three tests: business, turnover net of materials, and twelve months of clean compliance.
  • Compliance is what fails applications, and what loses the status later.
  • HMRC reviews it annually and automatically. Guard your filing calendar.

Gross payment status is the closest thing CIS has to a promotion: contractors pay you the full invoice, deductions stop, and your cash flow transforms overnight. HMRC grants it to businesses that pass three tests, and revokes it from businesses that stop passing them.

Business testGenuineconstruction work in the UK, run through a bank account
Turnover test£30knet of materials, per relevant person, or £100k for partnerships and companies
Compliance testCleanreturns and payments on time through the qualifying period

The three tests, in the order they trip people

  1. 1
    Compliance is the one that fails applications

    HMRC looks back over the previous twelve months at every return and payment across Self Assessment, CIS, PAYE and Corporation Tax. A handful of small lapses are tolerated within limits; a pattern is not.

  2. 2
    Turnover is measured on labour, net of materials

    Construction turnover excluding VAT and excluding the cost of materials. Firms that invoice materials heavily sometimes fall short despite a healthy top line.

  3. 3
    The business test is usually straightforward

    You carry out construction work in the UK, largely for contractors, and you operate through a bank account. Keep the account clean and business-only.

Same work, same invoices. The difference is entirely in when the tax is settled, not how much.

Keeping it is harder than getting it

HMRC reviews compliance annually. Late returns, late payments or a run of small slips can lose you the status, and the review is automatic rather than personal. Firms with gross status guard their filing calendar accordingly.

Before you apply

  • Twelve months of clean filing and payment history across every tax you touch
  • Turnover figures net of materials, evidenced from the books
  • A dedicated business bank account with construction income running through it
  • Any old debts settled or under an agreed time-to-pay arrangement
  • Records tidy enough to answer questions quickly if HMRC asks

The compliance test is the whole game, and it is exactly what a rails-based monthly rhythm produces as a by-product. See how the CIS side runs.

The compliance test, in detail

This is the test that decides most applications, and it is mechanical rather than subjective. HMRC looks back over the previous twelve months at your filing and payment record across every tax you touch. A small number of minor lapses are tolerated within published tolerances; a pattern of lateness is not.

ObligationWhat counts as a failure
Self Assessment returnFiled late
Self Assessment paymentPaid late, beyond the tolerance
CIS monthly returnsAny return late, including nil returns
CIS deductions paid overPaid after the deadline
PAYE returns and paymentsLate RTI submissions or late payment
Corporation TaxReturn or payment late, for companies

The lesson firms take from this: the twelve months before you apply should be spotless, deliberately.

  • Twelve months before
    Clean everything up

    Any historic debt settled or under an agreed arrangement. Every filing on time from here.

  • Application
    Apply online or by phone

    HMRC checks business, turnover and compliance.

  • Decision
    Usually weeks

    If refused, the letter states why, and you can appeal or reapply once the reason is fixed.

  • Granted
    Contractors pay you gross

    Verification by contractors now returns a gross rate.

  • Every year after
    Automatic review

    HMRC re-tests compliance annually. Slip and the status can be withdrawn.

The status is earned over a year and lost in a month. Treat the filing calendar as the asset it is.

If you are refused

The refusal letter tells you which test failed. Turnover shortfalls often reflect materials-heavy invoicing rather than a small business, and compliance failures are usually a handful of specific late items. Both are fixable, and reapplying after fixing them is normal rather than embarrassing.

What it is worth, honestly

Gross status does not reduce your tax. It changes when you pay it: instead of contractors withholding 20 percent as you go, you settle the whole liability through Self Assessment or Corporation Tax later. That is transformational for cash flow and dangerous for the undisciplined, because the money sitting in your account is not yours. Firms that thrive on gross status move the tax proportion into a separate account the day each payment lands.

Gross status suits you if

  • You forecast and set tax aside religiously
  • Cash flow is your constraint, not profitability
  • Your filing has been clean for a year
  • You are growing and funding work in advance

Think twice if

  • Tax money tends to get spent
  • Filing deadlines are already a struggle
  • Your turnover is close to the threshold
  • You would rather the deductions did the saving for you
