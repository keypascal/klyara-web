MTD for Income Tax is live: what actually happened this week

7 Apr 2026 · 10 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • From 6 April 2026, sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 must keep digital records and file quarterly.
  • The first quarterly updates fall due 7 August 2026. Penalty points apply to late updates, with a £200 fine at four points.
  • The £30,000 wave follows in April 2027 and the £20,000 wave in April 2028.
  • CIS subcontractors are measured on gross invoiced income, before deductions come off.

After two decades of consultation, deferral and false starts, Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is no longer a future event. The first mandation wave went live on 6 April 2026, and the self-employed tax year now runs on a quarterly rhythm for everyone over the first threshold.

What changed on the day

  • 6 Apr 2026
    Mandation begins

    Digital record keeping becomes a legal requirement for those in scope, from the first transaction of the new tax year.

  • 7 Aug 2026
    First updates due

    The opening quarter (6 April to 5 July) must be with HMRC by this date, filed from recognised software.

  • Apr 2027
    The 30k wave

    Qualifying income over £30,000 joins. This is the wave that catches most full-time subcontractors.

  • Apr 2028
    The 20k wave

    Essentially every full-time self-employed tradesperson is in by this point.

The timetable is now set in regulations rather than consultation documents, which is what makes this week different from every previous announcement.

What it means on site

Nothing about how much tax you pay changed this week. What changed is the shape of the year: records must be digital from the moment of purchase, four summaries go to HMRC through the year, and the January return is replaced by a final declaration. For a builder or subbie the practical translation is one habit and four diary dates: photograph every receipt when it exists, and file by the 7ths.

If you are over 50k and not set up

You are not the only one, but the clock is running: the first quarter's records are accruing now and the 7 August deadline will arrive quickly. Getting software and the receipt habit in place this month turns a compliance problem into a routine.

Our plain-English guide covers the whole system, and Klyara Solo exists precisely for this: MTD handled end to end for sole traders. See your date and what it means.

The penalty regime that switched on with it

FailureConsequence
Late quarterly updateOne penalty point. At four points, a £200 fine, then £200 per further late update
Points expiryA clean 24-month run, with everything outstanding filed, resets the counter
Late payment, day 153% of the unpaid tax
Still unpaid, day 30A further 3%
Beyond a month10% a year, plus interest running throughout

Filing and payment are penalised separately. If cash is tight, file on time regardless and arrange Time to Pay before the due date.

The priority when money is short

Filing late earns points and signals disorganisation. Paying late is expensive but mechanical. Always file on time, then talk to HMRC about payment. Time to Pay agreed before a deadline stops the percentage penalties.

Who is not affected yet

In scope now

  • Sole traders over £50,000 qualifying income
  • Landlords over the same threshold
  • Anyone who signed up voluntarily

Not yet

  • Sole traders under 50,000 (2027 or 2028)
  • Limited companies (a separate future regime)
  • Partnerships (deferred to a later date)

Limited companies are not part of this wave: MTD for Corporation Tax remains a future project. Partnerships are also deferred. If you incorporated last year, your quarterly obligation is on hold, but your bookkeeping habits should not be.

If you are in scope and not yet set up

  • Separate business bank account, this week
  • Recognised software with the bank feed connected
  • Receipt capture habit started immediately: the quarter is already accruing
  • Agent authorisation started now if someone else will file, because the postal step takes a week
  • Diary the four 7ths and 31 January
