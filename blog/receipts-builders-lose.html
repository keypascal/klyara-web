The receipts builders lose most, and what they really cost

1 Jul 2026 · 9 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Every lost receipt is tax relief you paid for and gave back.
  • Fuel, parking, cash merchant buys and tip fees are the usual casualties.
  • A bank statement proves money left, not what for, and does not support VAT reclaim.
  • Photograph at the counter. Ten seconds, and the paper's fate stops mattering.

Lost receipts feel like nothing in the moment and cost real money in April. Every one is an expense you paid for and cannot claim, tax relief donated back to HMRC. The usual suspects, in order of casualties:

The regulars

Fuel. Three fill-ups a week, thermal paper, van door pocket, sun. By spring the roll call of the missing runs to hundreds of pounds of relief. Parking. Two pounds here, four there; forty trips later it was a real number, unclaimed. Cash buys at the merchant. The Saturday-morning fixings run paid from your pocket is the single most-lost receipt in the trade. The cafe. Genuine subsistence on jobs away from your normal patch is claimable, and almost nobody keeps the evidence. Skip hire and tip fees. Paid on card, receipt emailed, buried under promotions, never seen again. Tool-shop impulse buys. The 30-pound blade set that lives in no ledger anywhere.

Why "the bank statement will do" does not

A statement line proves money left; it does not prove what for, or show the VAT. For VAT reclaim you need the receipt, and under Making Tax Digital the expectation is a digital record, not a shoebox reconstruction.

The ten-second cure

Photograph the receipt while it is still in your hand: counter, cab, cafe table. The app reads it, codes it, files it, and the paper's fate stops mattering. Every Klyara plan ships with exactly this, because the cheapest tax planning in Britain is simply keeping the evidence of money you already spent. See how the monthly rhythm works.

Every one of these is answered by the same ten-second habit.

Why MTD makes this urgent rather than optional

Digital record keeping means the expectation is a captured record, made close to the transaction, living in software. A shoebox reconstruction in January was always a bad idea; under quarterly filing it stops being practical at all.

The habit, in full

  • Photograph at the counter, before the receipt goes in a pocket
  • One app, always the same one, so nothing is scattered
  • Tag the job while you are standing there, if you job-cost
  • Never rely on the paper surviving: once photographed, its fate is irrelevant
  • Ask for a receipt on every cash purchase, without exception
