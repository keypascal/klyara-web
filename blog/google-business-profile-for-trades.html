Your Google Business Profile is the cheapest lead source you own

4 May 2026 · 11 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Your Google Business Profile is free and decides whether you appear in the map pack.
  • Primary category, service area, services, photos and reviews are the fields that move rankings.
  • Ask for a review after every completed job. Volume and recency both count.
  • Keep your name, address and phone identical across every directory. Inconsistency drags you down.

Ask a builder where their work comes from and they will say word of mouth. Watch what actually happens and you will see the recommendation, then the Google search of the firm's name, then a decision made on what came back. Your Google Business Profile is where that decision happens, it is free, and most trade profiles are half empty.

Cost£0free, forever, ten minutes to claim

The fields that actually move you

  1. 1
    Primary category, chosen precisely

    'Electrician' outranks 'contractor' for electrical searches. One field, enormous effect. Add secondary categories for genuine additional trades only.

  2. 2
    Service area, set honestly

    List the towns you truly cover. Overstating it dilutes relevance and wastes the enquiries you do get.

  3. 3
    Services and descriptions, filled in fully

    Every service you offer, described in the words customers use. This is free keyword coverage most competitors ignore.

  4. 4
    Photos, from real jobs, monthly

    Profiles with recent photos convert visibly better. Ten minutes a month, taken on the phone you already carry.

  5. 5
    Reviews, asked for every single time

    Volume and recency both count. A steady drip beats a burst, and answering every review signals a live business.

  6. 6
    Posts, weekly and short

    Small ranking signal, real credibility signal: a profile last updated in 2023 answers the wrong question about whether you are still trading.

Roughly how local homeowners arrive at a trade firm. Nine in ten look you up first, and the map pack is the front door.

The mistake that costs rankings

Inconsistent name, address and phone details across directories. Google cross-references them; conflicting information reads as unreliable. Fix the big ones once and it stays fixed.

Reviews are the fuel for all of it, and asking properly at the right moment is a system, not a personality trait. See how the review engine works.

The ranking factors you can actually control

Local ranking rests on three things Google states openly: relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot move your yard, but the other two are entirely in your hands, and most trade profiles leave both half done.

None of it is technical. All of it is boring, which is exactly why doing it beats most competitors.

Reviews: the compounding asset

  1. 1
    Ask every single completed job

    Not the ones you think went well. Every one. Systematic asking is what produces volume.

  2. 2
    Ask at the moment satisfaction peaks

    Job finished, site tidy, customer happy. An hour later is worse; a week later is far worse.

  3. 3
    Make it one tap

    A short text with a direct link. Anything requiring a search loses most people.

  4. 4
    Reply to every review, good and bad

    Speed and specificity both count. The audience is future customers reading how you handle criticism.

  5. 5
    Never gate or buy reviews

    Filtering who you ask by predicted rating breaches platform rules and UK consumer law, and detection is real.

Photos are the underrated field

Profiles with recent, real job photos convert visibly better than those with a logo and a stock image. Ten minutes a month of before-and-after shots taken on the phone you already carry outperforms most paid advertising a small trade firm could buy.

The weekly ten minutes

Do these and you beat most local competitors

  • Add two or three photos from the week's jobs
  • Post one short update: a job type, a seasonal note, an availability window
  • Reply to any new reviews and any questions
  • Check your opening hours are right, especially around holidays
  • Once a quarter, review your categories and services against what you actually sell
