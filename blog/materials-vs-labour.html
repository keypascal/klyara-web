CIS

Materials vs labour: the cheapest habit in construction

8 Jul 2026 · 9 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • CIS deductions come off labour only, never off properly itemised materials.
  • Lump everything into one line and the whole invoice suffers deduction.
  • On a £6,000 invoice, lumping costs you £400 of cash flow until refund time.
  • One extra line on your invoice template fixes it permanently.

Here is a one-line habit worth real money: split materials and labour on every invoice you send under CIS. That is the whole tip. The rest of this guide is why.

Deductions come off labour only

CIS tells contractors to deduct from the labour element of your invoice, not from materials you supplied at cost. Invoice £6,000 as one lump and a careful contractor has no choice but to deduct off the lot: £1,200 gone. Split it as £4,000 labour and £2,000 materials and the deduction is £800. The other £400 stays in your account instead of waiting a year to come home as a refund.

What counts as materials

Materials you actually bought for that job, at what they cost you: timber, fixings, plant hire for the job, fuel for plant. Not your travel, not your van, not a margin you would like to call materials. HMRC can ask for evidence, which is one more reason every merchant receipt should live in your books, not the glovebox.

For contractors, it cuts both ways

If you are the one paying subbies, you can only exclude materials from deduction when the split is genuine and evidenced. A subbie invoicing suspicious 50-50 splits on labour-heavy work is handing you their compliance risk. Push back; it protects you both.

Our invoicing setup does the split automatically and keeps the evidence attached, so the right money arrives untouched every time. Bookkeeping and CIS, handled.

Materials vs labour, at a glance

Usually materialsUsually labour
Timber, bricks, fixings, consumablesYour time and your crew's time
Plant hire without an operatorPlant hire with an operator
Manufactured items you supplySite supervision
Fuel used in plant, where itemisedTravel time

Materials must be genuine and evidenced by supplier invoices. Inflating the materials line to reduce deductions is exactly what HMRC checks for.

Making it permanent

  1. 1
    Fix the invoice template once

    Separate lines for labour and materials, with a subtotal for each. Every invoice from now on inherits it.

  2. 2
    Keep supplier invoices with the job

    Your materials figure must be evidenced. Job-coded receipts do this automatically.

  3. 3
    Never inflate the split

    Over-stating materials to reduce deductions is a compliance risk that dwarfs the cash benefit.

  4. 4
    Check your contractor applied it correctly

    Compare the deduction statement to what you invoiced. Errors happen and go uncorrected because nobody looks.

The compounding version

A subbie invoicing £8,000 a month with a third in materials recovers roughly £500 a month of cash flow simply by splitting the line properly. Over a year, that is a van deposit, and it required no extra work at all.
