VAT registration: when you must, and when you should anyway

15 Jul 2026 · 10 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Registration is compulsory above 90,000 pounds of taxable turnover in any rolling 12 months.
  • It is a rolling test, not a tax year: one big contract can register you overnight.
  • Voluntary registration can pay when you sell mostly to VAT-registered businesses.
  • Domestic-heavy firms usually lose out by registering early: your prices effectively rise.

The VAT registration threshold is 90,000 pounds of taxable turnover in any rolling 12 months. Not your accounting year, not the tax year: any 12-month window, checked continuously. That word "rolling" catches more builders than any other rule in this guide.

The trap

A strong autumn plus a big winter job can push your trailing 12 months over the line in February even though no single year ever looked scary. You have 30 days from the end of the month you crossed the line to register, and the clock does not care whether you noticed. Registering late means paying VAT you never charged, out of your own margin.

When registering early is the smart move

Counterintuitive but true: some builders should register before they must. If your customers are mainly VAT-registered firms, your prices are effectively ex VAT to them anyway, and registration lets you reclaim VAT on materials, plant, fuel and the van. Mostly domestic customers? The opposite: registration makes you 20 percent dearer than the unregistered firm quoting against you, so you stay under the line as long as you legitimately can.

The one-line rule

Commercial work: registering early usually pays. Domestic work: delay while you honestly can, but watch the rolling total monthly, not annually. Our monthly reports track your trailing 12 months as standard, so the threshold never arrives as a surprise. Ask us where you stand; it takes one look.

The rolling test, and the trap in it

The threshold is not measured by tax year or accounting year. At the end of every month you look back over the previous twelve and add up taxable turnover. Cross the line and you must register, with a short window to do it. This is why one unusually large contract can register a firm that has never come close before, and why firms with lumpy work should check the rolling figure monthly rather than annually.

The threshold is a moving twelve-month window. The third bar triggers compulsory registration even if the year overall looked normal.

Registering helps when

  • Most customers are VAT-registered businesses
  • You buy a lot of materials and plant
  • You work mostly B2B under the reverse charge
  • You want to look established to commercial clients

Registering hurts when

  • Most customers are homeowners
  • Your work is labour-heavy with low materials
  • You would have to absorb the VAT to stay competitive
  • Admin capacity is already stretched

The domestic pricing reality

Register while working mainly for homeowners and you either raise prices by a fifth or absorb the difference. Neither is comfortable, which is why domestic-heavy firms usually wait until the threshold forces the issue, while B2B subbies often register voluntarily and gain.

If you are approaching the line

  • Check the rolling twelve-month figure at the end of every month
  • Watch for one-off large contracts that push you over unexpectedly
  • Decide in advance whether you would register early or manage below the line legitimately
  • Never split a business artificially to stay under: HMRC treats disaggregation seriously
  • Get the reverse charge set up correctly from your first VAT invoice
