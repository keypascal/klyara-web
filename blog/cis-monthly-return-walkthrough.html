CIS

Filing a CIS monthly return: a walkthrough for new contractors

10 Aug 2026 · 11 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • The tax month runs 6th to 5th. Returns are due by the 19th, payment by the 22nd if paying electronically.
  • Verify every subbie with HMRC before the first payment. Guessing the rate makes the shortfall your bill.
  • Deduct from labour only. Materials itemised separately are never deducted from.
  • Nil months still need a return. Penalties start at £100 and climb from day one.

The month you first pay another tradesperson for construction work, you become a contractor in HMRC's eyes, and a monthly obligation switches on that never switches off again until you tell them it should. The good news: it is a small job done properly, and a nightmare done badly. Here is the whole thing, once, in the order it happens.

Filing deadline19thof every month, for the tax month ending the 5th
Late penalty£100immediately, then it climbs
Nil returnsStill dueno payments made still means a return

The monthly cycle, start to finish

  • 6th to 5th
    The tax month

    CIS runs 6th to 5th, not calendar months. Payments made on 4 March fall in the month ending 5 March.

  • Before first payment
    Verify every new subbie

    HMRC tells you the rate: 20% registered, 30% unregistered, or gross. Never guess it.

  • On payment
    Deduct from labour only

    Materials, plant hire with an operator and VAT are excluded. Split them clearly on the invoice.

  • Within 14 days of the 5th
    Issue deduction statements

    Every subbie, every month they were paid. This is their refund evidence and they are entitled to it.

  • By the 19th
    File the return

    Online, including a nil return if you paid nobody. Payment of the deductions is due by the 22nd if paying electronically.

One cycle, five moments. Miss the last one and penalties start at £100 and grow with time.

Verification, properly

Verification is not optional paperwork; it is the step that decides the rate. You give HMRC the subbie's name, unique taxpayer reference and National Insurance number, and they return a verification number and a deduction rate. Take the subbie's word for it instead and you carry the risk: if they were on 30% and you deducted 20, you owe HMRC the difference out of your own pocket, plus penalties. It takes two minutes and protects four figures.

SituationDeductionWhat it means for them
Registered subcontractor20% of labourNormal. Credited against their tax bill at year end.
Not registered with CIS30% of labourThey can fix it in minutes and drop to 20%.
Gross payment status0%They settle their own tax later. Verify it, do not assume it.

Rates apply to the labour element only, never to materials properly itemised.

Where firms actually get caught

The three that cost real money

Paying a subbie before verifying them; lumping materials into one line so the whole invoice suffers deduction; and forgetting a nil return in a quiet month. All three are avoidable, and all three appear in HMRC compliance checks constantly.

Contractor CIS is exactly the job that belongs on rails rather than in your memory. On Klyara Trade and Pro, verification, deductions, statements and the return itself run to a checklist every month, and you approve the summary from your phone. See how the monthly run works.

Deduction statements: the part subbies actually care about

Within fourteen days of the end of each tax month, every subcontractor you deducted from is entitled to a written statement showing the payments made and the amount withheld. It is a legal obligation, and from your subbie's side it is the single most important piece of paper you produce, because without it their refund claim becomes an argument with HMRC.

Must showWhy it matters
Your name and employer referenceIdentifies who withheld the money
The subcontractor's name and UTRTies the deduction to their tax record
The tax month ending datePlaces it in the right period
Gross amount, excluding VATThe starting figure
Cost of materialsThe part never deducted from
Amount deductedWhat went to HMRC on their behalf

Issue them monthly as a matter of routine. Subbies who chase you for statements every April are subbies who leave.

Paying HMRC, and the trap in month twelve

Deductions are paid over by the 22nd if you pay electronically, or the 19th by post. If your total PAYE and CIS liability is small, HMRC may allow quarterly payment, but the returns remain monthly regardless. Limited company subcontractors who suffer deductions themselves offset them against their own PAYE liabilities through the payroll system rather than waiting for a year-end refund, and that offset has to be claimed correctly each month or the money simply sits there.

The month-twelve trap

CIS offsets for limited companies must be claimed in-year through the EPS. Miss the deadline after the tax year ends and the offset can no longer be corrected through payroll; it becomes a repayment claim, which is slower and more fiddly. Diarise it like any other filing.

What an HMRC compliance check looks at

  1. 1
    Verification evidence

    Did you verify before the first payment, and can you produce the verification numbers?

  2. 2
    The materials split

    Are materials genuine, evidenced by supplier invoices, and proportionate to the work?

  3. 3
    Status

    Are these people genuinely self-employed, or are some of them employees in all but name?

  4. 4
    Statements

    Were they issued, on time, with the required information?

  5. 5
    Returns and payments

    Filed by the 19th, paid by the 22nd, including nil months?

None of that is difficult when it happens monthly on rails. All of it is painful when reconstructed two years later from a folder of remembered arrangements.

A clean CIS month

  • Every new subbie verified before payment
  • Materials itemised on every invoice
  • Statements issued within fourteen days
  • Return filed by the 19th, payment by the 22nd

The version that gets penalised

  • Rate assumed from what the subbie said
  • One lump-sum invoice line
  • Statements produced only when chased
  • Nil month skipped because nobody was paid
