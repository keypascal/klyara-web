What a missed call actually costs a building firm

8 Jun 2026 · 10 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Most trades miss around a third of their calls and have no record of it.
  • Under a fifth of callers leave a voicemail. The rest ring the next firm within minutes.
  • Do the arithmetic on your own numbers: missed calls times your conversion times your average job value.
  • Fix the leak before increasing advertising. Answering is cheaper than acquiring.

Every trade knows they miss calls. Almost none know the number, because a missed call leaves no trace: no voicemail, no message, no record that a person needed a builder at ten past two and rang someone else at quarter past.

Typical miss rate1 in 3for firms answering their own phone on site

The arithmetic, on your own numbers

  1. 1
    Count the enquiries you did answer last month

    Say twenty. Of those, you quoted twelve and won four.

  2. 2
    Assume you missed a third of the calls

    That is another ten calls, of which perhaps six were genuine enquiries.

  3. 3
    Apply your own conversion rate

    Six enquiries at your one-in-three quote-to-win rate is two more jobs.

  4. 4
    Multiply by your average job value

    Two jobs at 3,000 pounds is 6,000 pounds of work that existed and went elsewhere. Every month.

The three fixes, in order of cost

Cheapest first

  • Missed-call text back: an automatic message within seconds, so the caller knows you exist and will ring back
  • A proper business number that rings more than one handset, so the office or the other half can catch it
  • Human or AI answering for the hours you cannot pick up, which for most trades is most of the working day
  • Whatever you choose, measure it: a system that reports answered against missed turns a feeling into a number

Why it beats advertising

Marketing buys new calls at a price. Answering the phone converts calls you already paid for, at almost no cost. Fix the leak before turning the tap up.

Why voicemail no longer works

Voicemail was designed for a world where the caller had few alternatives. Today the search results that produced your number produced two more below it, and a homeowner with a leak is not leaving a message and waiting. Worse, a full or generic voicemail signals a firm that is either too busy or too disorganised, which is exactly the impression a new customer is scanning for.

What good looks like

  • Answered within a few rings, in your firm's name
  • Caller's details captured with the job type
  • A text confirmation to the caller
  • A summary on your phone before you finish the task

What most firms have

  • Rings out on site
  • Generic voicemail nobody checks until evening
  • No record the call happened
  • No idea how many were missed

Measure before you spend on marketing

Advertising buys new calls at a price. Answering converts calls you already paid for at almost no cost. Any firm considering an ad budget should first find out what proportion of its existing calls go unanswered, because that number usually settles the argument.

You cannot miss what you never knew rang. That is precisely why this leak survives for years in otherwise well-run firms.
