Getting paid

Deposits and stage payments: funding the job with the customer's money

9 Mar 2026 · 10 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Never fund a customer's job from your own overdraft. Staging is standard commercial practice.
  • Tie each stage to visible progress on site, not to a calendar date.
  • Keep the final balance small so a snag cannot become a hostage situation.
  • Put the schedule in the quote, not in a conversation after acceptance.

A builder funding a customer's kitchen out of their own overdraft is doing unpaid banking with all of the risk and none of the interest. Staged payments are not cheeky; they are the standard commercial arrangement in every construction market on earth, and customers accept them when the structure is explained plainly.

A structure that works on domestic jobs

  • On acceptance
    Deposit

    Covers materials ordering and secures the slot in your diary. Nothing is bought before it clears.

  • First fix complete
    Stage one

    Visible, verifiable progress. The customer can see exactly what they are paying for.

  • Second fix complete
    Stage two

    Keeps you cash positive through the most expensive part of the job.

  • Practical completion
    Balance

    Kept modest deliberately, so a snag never turns into a hostage situation.

  • Snags cleared
    Any retention

    Domestic jobs rarely need one, but a small holdback can be a fair compromise on larger works.

The principle: money arrives shortly before you spend it, not months after.

The line that makes it easy to ask

'Payments are staged so you only ever pay for work that is already done, and I never ask you to fund the next stage before you can see the last one.' It reframes staging as protection for them, which is exactly what it is.

Getting it agreed without friction

Do these five things

  • Put the schedule in the quote, not in a conversation after acceptance
  • Tie every stage to something visible on site, not to a calendar date
  • Invoice the moment a stage completes, same day, from the phone
  • Set out payment terms in days and state the statutory interest position once, calmly
  • Take payment by bank transfer or card, and make the link easy to click

Consumer rights apply on domestic contracts and deposits must be handled fairly, so keep the amounts proportionate and be clear about cancellation. Structured properly, staging is the single biggest cash-flow improvement most small firms can make in a week. See how invoicing and chasing run.

The pattern is consistent: the bigger the job, the smaller the deposit percentage and the more frequent the stages.

Why the final payment should be small

A 30 percent balance gives an unhappy customer enormous leverage over a minor snag. A 10 percent balance keeps everyone reasonable, because the amount in dispute is proportionate to the problem.

Handling the awkward conversation

What to say

  • 'Payments are staged so you only pay for work already done'
  • 'The deposit covers your materials, which I order the day it clears'
  • 'I invoice each stage the day it completes, so there are no surprises'
  • 'Everything is in the quote, including the schedule'

What not to say

  • 'I need a deposit because I am a bit short'
  • 'Most people pay something up front'
  • 'We can sort the money out later'
  • 'Do not worry about the paperwork'

Customers accept staging when it is presented as the normal way construction works, because it is. They resist when it sounds like a cash-flow rescue, because that raises the question of whether you will still be trading in six weeks.

Consumer protection: what to get right

On domestic contracts

  • Give the required cancellation notice for contracts agreed at the customer's home, and do not start within the cancellation period without written agreement
  • Keep deposits proportionate to the materials and mobilisation they fund
  • State clearly what the deposit covers and what happens to it if either side cancels
  • Provide a written quote and terms before taking money
  • Offer a payment method with a paper trail: bank transfer or card, never cash for deposits

The customer is not funding your business. They are paying for work already done, one stage at a time. Explained that way, almost nobody argues.
