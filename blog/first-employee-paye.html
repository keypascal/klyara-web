Taking on your first employee: PAYE, pensions and RTI

7 Aug 2026 · 11 min read · by the Klyara team

30 second versionBreak it down for me

  • Budget 20 to 25 percent above gross once employer's NI, pension and holiday are counted.
  • Register as an employer and set up payroll before the start date, not after.
  • Auto-enrolment duties begin with the first eligible payday.
  • Check right to work before day one: it is a legal requirement with real penalties.

Subbies invoice you; employees are a different universe. The day your first proper hire starts, a set of obligations switches on that repeats every single payday, forever. Here is the map.

Before day one

Register as an employer with HMRC (allow a couple of weeks). Get employers' liability insurance, which is a legal requirement with fines for every uncovered day. Confirm the right to work. Issue a written statement of employment terms, required from day one, not "when things settle down".

Every payday: RTI

Real Time Information means you report each pay run to HMRC on or before the day wages are paid. Weekly-paid crew means fifty-two deadlines a year. Late or wrong submissions attract penalties, and construction pay, with its hours, overtime, CIS-to-PAYE conversions and weather weeks, is exactly the kind that goes wrong in spreadsheets.

Pensions, minimum wage, the quiet duties

Auto-enrolment applies from the first eligible employee: assess, enrol, contribute, keep records. Minimum wage rates move every April, and apprentice and age-band rules trip people. Holiday pay accrues from day one, including for casuals.

The employer's National Insurance surprise

Budget for employer's NI on top of gross pay, softened by the Employment Allowance for most small firms. The true cost of a hire is comfortably above the number you agreed on the phone; know it before you shake hands.

Our payroll runs PAYE crew and CIS subbies in one rhythm: payslips, RTI, pension files and deduction statements, from 5 pounds a payslip. One pay run, everyone right.

The seven steps, in order

  1. 1
    Decide the role and the rate

    Written down before you advertise: tasks, hours, and what good looks like after ninety days.

  2. 2
    Check right to work

    Before day one. It is a legal requirement with substantial penalties for getting it wrong.

  3. 3
    Register as an employer with HMRC

    Do this ahead of the start date: it is not instant.

  4. 4
    Set up payroll software

    Or hand it to a bookkeeper. RTI submissions are due on or before every payday.

  5. 5
    Get employers' liability insurance

    A legal requirement from the first employee, with daily fines for going without.

  6. 6
    Sort the pension

    Auto-enrolment duties start with the first eligible payday. Assess, enrol, contribute, record.

  7. 7
    Issue a written statement of employment particulars

    On or before day one. It is the legal minimum, and it prevents most disputes.

The Employment Allowance

Most small employers can reduce their employer's National Insurance bill by an annual allowance, claimed through payroll. It is routinely missed by firms running payroll themselves, and it materially changes the arithmetic above.

Ongoing, once they start

  • RTI submission on or before every payday, without exception
  • Pension contributions paid within the statutory deadlines
  • Holiday accrual tracked from day one, including for casual hours
  • Minimum wage checked at every birthday and every April
  • Records kept for six years, payslips issued every pay period
