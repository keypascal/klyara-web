# The five that need a decision

## 1. The testimonials — score 20 🔴
**Why it tops the list:** it is the only item that is simultaneously a legal exposure, a regulatory
exposure during a licence application, and a direct contradiction of the thing you sell.

Fake reviews and testimonials are a banned practice under the Digital Markets, Competition and
Consumers Act 2024, enforceable by the CMA. You are about to put a practice licence application in
front of a professional body with conduct rules on honest marketing.

**Mitigation:** remove today. Replacement copy is in `docs/07 §Option B`. It takes ten minutes.
**Residual risk after mitigation:** near zero. This is the cheapest risk on the register to close.

## 2. AML supervision slipping — score 15
Every pound of revenue sits behind it. ICB states 28 days; HMRC direct states up to 45. If the
application goes in late September you are into November before you can trade, and the founding
waitlist has to hold people for six weeks longer than they expected.

**Mitigation:** apply this week, via ICB (cheaper, faster, includes supervision — `docs/17 §1`).
Write the application date on the wall. If it passes 28 days, chase weekly.
**Contingency:** the waitlist keeps working. Keep publishing regardless — the pipeline is being built
either way, and a founding rate held for longer is a stronger offer, not a weaker one.

## 3. Craig is the entire product — score 15
Every channel in this pack is fronted by one person. That is exactly why it works, and it is a single
point of failure for both delivery and marketing.

**Mitigation:** the continuity arrangement is not paperwork, it is the answer to this risk. Get it
agreed with a partner accountant (`docs/11 §8`). Separately, document the monthly client process so
someone else could run it — `docs/12` is the start of that.
**Watch for:** the first week Craig is ill and nothing ships. That is the rehearsal.

## 4. No founders' agreement — score 15
Not a marketing risk, and I am flagging it anyway because it kills more small firms than competition
does — and **marketing that works makes it more urgent, not less.** The moment there is revenue,
clients and a brand with value, the cost of not having agreed who owns what goes up sharply.

**Mitigation:** heads of terms this month. It does not need to be elaborate; it needs to exist.

## 5. Capacity before hiring — score 12
The KPI model says you pass the 15-client hire trigger around **April 2027** and are over the 20–25
ceiling by month 12 — on assumptions that are deliberately not aggressive.

**Mitigation:** decide the waitlist policy *before* you need it. Holding a founding-rate waitlist is
a far better outcome than taking on client 26 badly and losing client 4. Start the recruitment
conversation one month before the model says you cross 15.

# The ones that quietly do the damage

**Marketing stops in week 4.** This is the most common failure mode for a plan like this, and it
never announces itself. The tell is two consecutive missed Wednesday community slots. `docs/16` has a
fallback plan naming exactly what to protect and what to drop — the whole point of it is to make
"a bad week" survivable rather than terminal.

**Zero reviews at day 90.** Reviews are the biggest single gap in the AI-visibility analysis, the
thing 65% of consumers use to reject businesses, and the thing 47% use as a hard filter below twenty.
And they cost nothing but the asking. Three clients with no review request is a process failure, not
a client failure.

**A published tax figure is wrong.** A bookkeeping firm that publishes an incorrect CIS penalty
figure has a credibility problem that no amount of good content fixes. `docs/17 §1` flags which
figures I verified at source and which I could not — check CIS340 before publishing penalty amounts.

# What I'd worry about that isn't on the list

**The pack is bigger than the business.** Twenty documents, ten web pages, 109 assets and a 90-day
plan is a lot of apparatus for a firm with no clients. There is a real risk of *doing the marketing*
instead of *doing the business* — of a satisfying Tuesday spent scheduling posts while nobody has
been rung.

The counterweight is in the plan: the two-hour steady state, the fallback list, and the fact that the
single highest-return activity in the whole pack — twenty emails to local accountants — takes an
afternoon and needs none of the rest of it.

**If you are ever choosing between a post and a phone call, make the call.**
