*Prepared 28 August 2026, against launch Monday 14 September 2026 and day 90 Saturday 12 December 2026.*

*A business partner's job includes saying the uncomfortable thing once, clearly, and then getting on
with it. This is that.*

**Scoring:** Likelihood and Impact are 1–5. Score is the product. Anything **15 or above needs a
decision this month**. **Owner** is the named person who acts, not the person who worries.
**Trigger** is the observable event that says the risk is now live — if it fires, act that week.

# The five that need a decision this month

Four of these score 15 or above. The fifth, capacity, scores 12 and is on the list anyway because the
lead time on hiring is longer than its score suggests — by the time it fires it is too late to act.

## 1. The testimonials — score 20 🔴
**Why it tops the list:** it is the only item that is simultaneously a legal exposure, a regulatory
exposure during a licence application, and a direct contradiction of the thing you sell.

Fake reviews and testimonials are a banned practice under the Digital Markets, Competition and
Consumers Act 2024, enforceable by the CMA. You are about to put a practice licence application in
front of a professional body with conduct rules on honest marketing.

**Owner:** Key. **Mitigation:** remove on Wednesday 2 September. Replacement copy is in
`docs/07 §Option B`. It takes ten minutes.
**Trigger:** they are still live on Thursday 3 September.
**Residual risk after mitigation:** near zero. This is the cheapest risk on the register to close.

## 2. AML supervision slipping — score 15
Every pound of revenue sits behind it. **Until supervision is in place Klyara cannot lawfully carry on
bookkeeping by way of business at all** — not for a fee, not for a deposit, not for a favour. The plan
is: apply to the ICB on **Tuesday 1 September**, 28-day stated processing, supervision expected around
**Tuesday 29 September**. HMRC direct registration is £300 up front, £400 per premises annually and up
to 45 days, which is why the ICB route is the recommendation (`docs/17 §1`).

If the application goes in late, or the 28 days runs long, you are into November before you can trade
and the founding waitlist has to hold people six weeks longer than they expected.

**Owner:** Craig. **Mitigation:** apply 1 September. Write the date on the wall. Chase weekly from day
28. Get the PII quotes in the same week, because the licence is conditional on cover being in place.
**Trigger:** no ICB acknowledgement by Tuesday 8 September, or no licence by Tuesday 29 September.
**Contingency:** the waitlist keeps working. Keep publishing regardless — the pipeline is being built
either way, and a founding rate held for longer is a stronger offer, not a weaker one. Every "hire us"
CTA stays on `⛔ HOLD` until the licence number is in hand.

## 3. Craig is the entire product — score 15
Every channel in this pack is fronted by one person. That is exactly why it works, and it is a single
point of failure for both delivery and marketing.

**Owner:** Craig. **Mitigation:** the continuity arrangement is not paperwork, it is the answer to
this risk. Agree it with a partner accountant (`docs/11 §8`) — the twenty-firm outreach in week 2 is
the natural place to raise it. Separately, document the monthly client process so someone else could
run it; `docs/12` is the start of that.
**Trigger:** the first week Craig is ill and nothing ships. That is the rehearsal, and it will happen
before day 90.

## 4. No founders' agreement — score 15
Not a marketing risk, and it is flagged anyway because it kills more small firms than competition
does — and **marketing that works makes it more urgent, not less.** The moment there is revenue,
clients and a brand with value, the cost of not having agreed who owns what goes up sharply.

**Owner:** Key. **Mitigation:** heads of terms this month, covering equity split, who owns the client
relationships, what happens if one founder leaves, and who signs what. It does not need to be
elaborate; it needs to exist.
**Trigger:** unsigned on Wednesday 30 September.

## 5. Capacity before hiring — score 12
The KPI model says you pass the 15-client hire trigger around **April 2027** and are over the 20–25
ceiling by month 12 — on assumptions that are deliberately not aggressive.

**Owner:** Craig. **Mitigation:** decide the waitlist policy *before* you need it. Holding a
founding-rate waitlist is a far better outcome than taking on client 26 badly and losing client 4.
Start the recruitment conversation one month before the model says you cross 15.
**Trigger:** twelve live clients with no recruitment conversation started.

# The ones that quietly do the damage

**Marketing stops in week 4.** This is the most common failure mode for a plan like this, and it
never announces itself. The tell is two consecutive missed Wednesday community slots. `docs/16` has a
fallback plan naming exactly what to protect and what to drop — the point of it is to make a bad week
survivable rather than terminal.

**Zero reviews at day 90.** Reviews are the biggest single gap in the AI-visibility analysis
(`docs/17 §4`). BrightLocal has 65% of consumers citing review-related reasons for rejecting a
business and 47% refusing to use one with fewer than twenty reviews (`docs/17 §3`) — which is why the
target in `docs/06 §9` is twenty-plus, not ten. And they cost nothing but the asking. Three clients
with no review request is a process failure, not a client failure.

**A published tax figure is wrong.** A bookkeeping firm that publishes an incorrect CIS penalty figure
has a credibility problem that no amount of good content fixes. `docs/17 §1` flags which figures were
verified at source and which were not — check CIS340 before publishing penalty amounts, and log the
date you checked.

# What I'd worry about that isn't on the list

**The pack is bigger than the business.** Twenty-two documents, sixteen web pages and a 90-day plan is
a lot of apparatus for a firm with no clients. There is a real risk of *doing the marketing* instead
of *doing the business* — of a satisfying Tuesday spent scheduling posts while nobody has been rung.

The counterweight is in the plan: the two-hour steady state, the fallback list, and the fact that the
single highest-return activity in the whole pack — twenty emails to local accountants — takes an
afternoon and needs none of the rest of it.

**If you are ever choosing between a post and a phone call, make the call.**
