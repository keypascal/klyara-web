# 23 — Outreach and partners
### The first fifty conversations. Scripts you can read off the screen.

---

## Why this document exists

Content and SEO work. They work in month four. The 90-day plan (docs/16) needs clients in month
one, and the only thing that produces clients in month one is talking to people.

This is the outbound half of the business, written out in full. Nothing in here is a strategy; it
is all sentences you can send today. Where a script has a bracket, fill the bracket. Where it does
not, do not improve it — every one of these is written short on purpose, because short gets read.

**One rule before anything else.** Outbound in this trade works because you are useful before you
are commercial. Every sequence below leads with something the recipient can use whether or not
they ever speak to you. That is not a tactic, it is the reason it works — the guides and the twelve
free calculators on the site exist so that outbound has something to give away.

---

# PART 1 — Who to talk to, in priority order

| Rank | Who | Why them | How many are reachable in 90 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sole-trader subbies you already know | Warm, quick, cheap to serve, gives you reviews fast | 10–30 |
| 2 | Small contractors paying 2–8 subbies | The Trade tier. Where the money is. | 40–80 |
| 3 | General accountants without a construction desk | Referrals, repeatable, no fee competition | 15–25 firms |
| 4 | Builders merchants and tool hire counters | Physical footfall of exactly your client | 8–12 branches |
| 5 | Quantity surveyors and small architects | Adjacent professionals, same client, no overlap | 10–15 |
| 6 | Local Facebook trade groups | Free, high volume, low conversion, high visibility | 6–10 groups |
| 7 | Bookkeepers who do not do CIS | Overflow work, and they exist in numbers | 10–20 |

Work the list top down. Rank 1 and 2 produce clients. Ranks 3–5 produce a pipeline that still
delivers in month nine, which is why they cannot wait until month nine to start.

# PART 2 — LinkedIn

## The connection request

Twenty words maximum. LinkedIn caps the note and a long one reads like a pitch.

**To a contractor or subcontractor**

> Hi [Name] — you're building around Guildford by the look of it. I do the back office for
> construction firms here. Worth knowing each other.

**To an accountant**

> Hi [Name] — you'll have builders on your books somewhere. I do construction-only bookkeeping in
> Guildford, CIS and reverse charge all day. Worth connecting.

**To a QS or architect**

> Hi [Name] — you see the same Surrey firms I do, from the other end. Guildford, construction
> bookkeeping. Happy to connect.

**Never** send a connection request with a pitch in it. The note buys the connection; the
connection buys the right to send a message later.

## The sequence — contractors and subcontractors

Four messages over three weeks. Stop at any reply, and stop entirely after message four whatever
happens. There is no message five.

**Day 0 — accepted, no pitch**

> Thanks for connecting, [Name]. No pitch — I just like knowing who's doing what locally.
> What sort of work are you mostly on at the moment?

**Day 4 — give something**

> [Name] — most subbies find out they're on 30% rather than 20% when the money lands short, which
> is about two months too late to do anything about it.
>
> There's a CIS calculator on our site that shows what should actually reach your bank, materials
> split out, and flags the 20/30% thing. Free, no sign-up: klyara.com/tools.html
>
> Useful either way. Nothing needed back.

**Day 11 — the specific question**

> Quick one, [Name] — do you file monthly CIS returns yourself, or does your accountant?
>
> Asking because we're seeing a lot of firms where the accountant does the year end and nobody
> really owns the monthly, so the 19th is a scramble. If that's not you, ignore me.

**Day 20 — the close, and the exit**

> Last one from me, [Name]. If the monthly CIS and VAT side is ever more hassle than it's worth,
> we do it for a fixed monthly fee. Construction only, nothing else. Fifteen minutes on the phone,
> no pitch, and if you're fine as you are I'll say so.
>
> If it's not relevant, no problem at all — I'll leave you be. Good luck with the [job/season].

That last line matters more than the rest. A clean exit is the reason people come back months
later, and some of them will. Log the date you closed the sequence and try again in six months.

## The sequence — accountants

Different tone entirely. You are not selling, you are offering to take work they do not want.

**Day 0**

> Thanks for connecting, [Name]. Most practices I speak to have four or five builders on the books
> and the monthly CIS is nobody's favourite job.
>
> That's all we do — CIS, reverse charge, retentions, the monthly grind, construction only. We work
> alongside accountants rather than competing. Year end stays with you.

**Day 6**

> [Name] — genuine question rather than a pitch. How many construction clients do you have on the
> books, and is the monthly CIS side something you actively want or something you inherited?
>
> The reason I ask: most general practices I speak to have four or five builders, and the CIS
> returns are twenty per cent of the hassle for two per cent of the fee.

**Day 15 — the offer, made concrete**

> [Name], here's the shape of it if it's ever useful.
>
> You keep the year end, the CT600 and the relationship. You get 10% of their monthly fee back,
> every month, for as long as they stay with us — on a Trade client that's £19.90 a month, £238.80
> a year, for work you didn't want. And you get clean books in April instead of a shoebox.
>
> What we take is the monthly bookkeeping, CIS returns and VAT. We never offer a client you
> introduce anything you already do for them, and that's a term of the agreement, not a promise.
>
> Worth twenty minutes? Coffee in Guildford or a call, whichever suits.

**Day 30 — the useful exit**

> [Name] — I'll stop chasing. One thing that might be worth having regardless: we wrote up the
> reverse charge end user rules and the gross payment status tests properly, because nobody else
> seems to have. klyara.com/vat-reverse-charge-end-user-declaration.html
>
> Send it to whichever client keeps asking. Door's open if the outsourcing idea ever comes round.

## Posting versus messaging

Messaging produces clients; posting makes messaging work. Someone who has already seen your posts
answers a message that a cold contact ignores. The content calendar (docs/03) covers what to post.
The rule for outbound is simply: **do not start a LinkedIn sequence in a week where you have posted
nothing.**

# PART 3 — Cold email

Cold email to businesses is lawful in the UK under PECR when it is business-to-business and the
recipient can opt out easily — but be careful: a **sole trader** and an unincorporated partnership
count as individual subscribers, so consent rules bite. In practice that means:

- Limited companies and LLPs: legitimate interest, fine, include an opt-out.
- Sole traders: do not cold email. Use LinkedIn, phone, or in person.
- Every email carries a real postal address and a one-line unsubscribe.
- Keep a suppression list and honour it forever.

Ten emails on a Tuesday and ten on a Thursday, twenty a week, sent one at a time from your normal
mailbox. That matches the blocks in PART 7. Not a platform, not a merge field that can misfire, and
never a BCC list.

## Sequence A — contractors, three emails

**Email 1 · Subject: Your CIS return and the 19th** *(28 characters)*

> [Name],
>
> Are your CIS returns something you think about on the 18th?
>
> Most firms your size are. The return, the statements, the one subbie who hasn't sent his invoice,
> all of it landing in the same twenty-four hours every month.
>
> We file on the 12th. Construction only, bookkeeping and CIS, fixed monthly fee, based in
> Guildford.
>
> Not asking for anything today. If the 19th ever stops being manageable, the number's below.
>
> Craig · Klyara · 020 8058 1496 · klyara.com
> [postal address] · Reply "no thanks" and I won't email again.

**Email 2 · four days later · Subject: CIS on materials** *(16 characters)*

> [Name] — one thing worth checking whether or not you ever call us.
>
> CIS comes off labour. It does not come off materials, plant hire or consumables. If your
> subcontractors' invoices don't show the split, the deduction usually goes on the whole thing, and
> that's their money and your paperwork.
>
> We wrote it up properly here: klyara.com/cis-materials-deduction.html
>
> Takes three minutes to read and it's the single most common thing we fix.
>
> [signature + opt-out]

**Email 3 · nine days later · Subject: Last one from me** *(16 characters)*

> [Name] — I'll leave it there.
>
> If the monthly side ever gets away from you — CIS, VAT, the reverse charge, chasing statements —
> we do it for a fixed fee from £59 a month plus VAT, and we only do construction. Fifteen minutes
> on the phone, no pitch, and if you're fine as you are I'll tell you.
>
> Otherwise good luck with the year. I won't email again.
>
> [signature + opt-out]

# PART 4 — The phone

The phone converts better than everything else on this page combined and almost nobody does it.
Ten calls on a Monday and ten on a Wednesday, twenty a week, **08:00–08:30** or 4:30 to 6pm. That
matches the blocks in PART 7. Builders answer early and late; they do not answer at 11am because they
are on a roof. The calls sit at 08:00 rather than 07:30 because 07:30 Monday is the anchor-post slot
in `docs/06 §2` — that post is written and scheduled in advance, so it holds the slot, and the calls
start the moment it goes out.

## The cold call — thirty seconds to earn the next ninety

> "Morning — is that [Name]? It's Craig at Klyara in Guildford. Have I caught you on a roof?"
>
> *(If bad: "No problem — better time later in the week?" and get one. Then go.)*
>
> "Thirty seconds. Can I ask you one thing and then I'll get off the phone? Who does your monthly
> CIS returns at the moment?
>
> Reason I'm asking: we do the books and the CIS for building firms, that's all we do, and I'd
> rather know who's who locally than post about it on LinkedIn."

Then shut up. The answer tells you everything.

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "My accountant" | "Fair enough — and do they chase the statements and check the materials split, or just file it?" |
| "I do them myself" | "Most people do. How long does the 19th take you, honestly?" |
| "My wife does them" | "Comes up more than any other answer. Does she want to?" |
| "We don't have subbies" | "Then you don't need me for CIS. Are you VAT registered? The reverse charge is the other one that catches people." |
| "Not interested" | "No problem at all. Can I send you one link? It's a CIS calculator, it's free, no sign-up. Then I'll leave you alone." |

**The ask, if it is going well**

> "Look, I'm not going to sell you anything on the phone. Fifteen minutes, either a call or I'll
> come to you, and I'll tell you what your setup actually needs and what it would cost. If that's
> nothing, I'll say so. Any good?"

## Voicemail — under twenty seconds

> "[Name], it's Craig at Klyara in Guildford. We do the books and the CIS for building firms.
> Ringing round local trades, nothing urgent. 020 8058 1496 if you want it, otherwise I'll try you
> another time. Cheers."

No pitch on a voicemail. The only job is that the name is familiar next time.

## The inbound call — someone rang you

Answer within three rings, always with the same line, because it makes you sound bigger than you
are.

> "Klyara, Craig speaking."

Then, in order:

1. **Get the name and the number first.** "Before we get into it — can I take a number in case we
   get cut off?" Do this in the first thirty seconds. Calls drop, and an anonymous lost enquiry is
   the most expensive thing that happens all week.
2. **Ask what prompted the call.** Not "how can I help" — "what's made you pick up the phone
   today?" The answer is the whole diagnosis.
3. **Do not quote a price in the first four minutes.** docs/09 has the full script.
4. **Book the call before you hang up.** "I'll send you a link now while we're on the phone."
5. **Send the follow-up within one hour.** Non-negotiable. A builder who rings three bookkeepers
   at lunchtime signs with whichever one replied first, and by teatime the other two are wasting
   their time.

# PART 5 — The partner programme

## The offer, in one paragraph

> You keep the year end and the relationship. You get **10% of their monthly fee, every month, for
> as long as they stay with us** — not a one-off finder's fee — paid on the 15th against a
> self-billing statement we send you. We do their monthly bookkeeping, CIS and VAT, and we will not
> offer them any service you already provide, which is a term of the agreement, not a promise.

## Why 10% ongoing rather than a lump sum

A one-off payment gets you one referral. A revenue share gets you a partner who thinks about you
in October. On a Trade client at £199 a month ex VAT, 10% is £19.90 a month, £238.80 a year, every
year they stay. For an accountant with four builders on the books, sending all four is about £955 a
year for work they did not want, plus books they can actually close in April.

The maths on your side: you give up 10% of the fee to acquire a client at no acquisition cost, on a
warm introduction from someone they already trust. It is the cheapest client you will ever get, and
an introduced client is the least likely to leave.

## Who to sign up, and what to say

### General accountants — the primary target

Fifteen to twenty-five firms within twenty miles. The pitch is in PART 2 and PART 3. Get to a
coffee, and bring a one-pager (`assets/sales/Klyara-For-Accountants-Partnership.pdf`).

**The objection you will get, and the answer**

> "How do I know you won't take the year end off me?"
>
> "Because it's in the agreement, in writing, and because I don't want it. I don't do CT600s, I'm
> not set up for them, and the day I start doing them is the day this whole arrangement stops
> working for me. My business model is thirty monthly fees, not four annual ones."

### Builders merchants and tool hire

Travis Perkins, Jewson, Selco, Howdens, local independents, and the tool hire counters. The
trade counter at 7am is standing in front of your entire client list.

**What the branch gets:** something to give their account customers that isn't another discount.
A free CIS health check for account holders, or a fifteen-minute slot at whatever trade morning
they run. **What you get:** a stack of A5 cards by the till and a line in their trade account
welcome pack.

**What to say to the branch manager**

> "I'm not selling you anything. I do the books for building firms — CIS, VAT, the boring end. Half
> your trade account customers get the CIS bit wrong and it costs them money. Can I leave a few
> cards, and would a free session for your account holders be any use to you? You'd be giving your
> customers something, and I'd be meeting some builders."

Track which branch each enquiry came from — different phone number or a different card per branch
— or you will never know which one works.

### Quantity surveyors and small architects

Ten to fifteen locally. Same client, zero overlap, and they hear about cash flow problems before
anybody else does. No revenue share needed here as a rule — a reciprocal referral relationship
works better, because you will meet firms who need a QS long before they need an architect.

### Bookkeepers who do not do CIS

There are more of these than you would think, and they are the least competitive partner of the
lot. A general bookkeeper with one building client is usually looking for a way to hand it over
without losing face.

> "If you've got a construction client that's more trouble than it's worth, I'll take it and pay
> you 10% of the fee for as long as they're with me. You keep everything else they might send you."

## The partner terms — put these in writing

1. 10% of the net monthly fee, paid monthly, for the life of the client relationship. Ten per cent
   is the rate everywhere, in every document and every conversation. Do not negotiate it upwards
   for one firm, because the second partner will find out.
2. Paid by bank transfer on the 15th, against a self-billing statement we send you.
3. Klyara will not offer year-end accounts, CT600, or personal tax to an introduced client where
   the partner provides those services.
4. Either side can end the arrangement with thirty days' notice; commission on existing introduced
   clients continues while those clients remain.
5. The client is told there is a referral arrangement. Always. It is a professional conduct
   requirement and it costs nothing.
6. No commission on a client who was already in conversation with Klyara — checked against the CRM
   at the point of introduction, in good faith, and the benefit of any doubt goes to the partner.

## The partner tracker

Add a tab to the KPI workbook, or a simple sheet:

| Partner | Type | First contact | Status | Introductions | Live clients | Monthly commission | Last contact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Rule: no partner goes more than eight weeks without contact.** A dormant partner is worth
nothing, and the reactivation email is two lines.

> "[Name] — nothing needed, just keeping in touch. The [reverse charge / MTD] thing is coming up
> fast for anyone with builders on the books, so if any of yours get stuck on it, send them over.
> How's your year going?"

# PART 6 — Facebook groups and forums

Six to ten local and trade groups. Guildford and Surrey community groups, Surrey trades groups,
UK-wide builder and subcontractor groups.

**The rules, in order of how badly people get them wrong**

1. **Read the group rules before posting anything.** Most ban promotion outright. That is fine —
   the value is in answering questions, not posting.
2. **Never post an advert.** Ever. One promotional post gets you removed and you cannot get back in.
3. **Answer CIS and VAT questions properly and completely, with no call to action.** Your profile
   says who you are. That is the advert.
4. **Never give specific tax advice to a named person's specific situation in public.** General
   principle, then "worth checking with whoever does your books — happy to point you at the
   guidance either way."
5. **If someone asks for a recommendation, let someone else name you.** If nobody does, comment
   once, plainly: "We do this — Klyara, Guildford, construction only. Happy to answer it here for
   free either way."

**Time budget: twenty minutes on two days a week**, matching the Tuesday and Thursday blocks in
PART 7. That is forty minutes for eight useful answers, and it is one of the best-value forty
minutes in this document. It pays nothing in the week you do it and everything three months later,
which is why it starts in week one and not when you need clients.

# PART 7 — The weekly outbound rhythm

Book it in the diary as a recurring block or it will not happen.

| Day | Block | What |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | 08:00–08:30 | 10 cold calls |
| Mon | 16:30–17:00 | LinkedIn: 15 connection requests, work the sequence |
| Tue | 07:30–08:00 | 10 cold emails (limited companies only) |
| Tue | 20 min | Facebook groups |
| Wed | 08:00–08:30 | 10 cold calls |
| Wed | 16:00–17:00 | One partner meeting or coffee |
| Thu | 07:30–08:00 | 10 cold emails, follow-ups |
| Thu | 20 min | Facebook groups |
| Fri | 07:30–08:30 | Follow up everyone who half-answered this week |
| Fri | 16:00–16:30 | Update the tracker. Count the numbers below. |

## The weekly numbers

| Metric | Weekly target |
|---|---|
| Cold calls made | 20 |
| Conversations of over a minute | 6 |
| Cold emails sent | 20 |
| LinkedIn connections sent | 15 |
| LinkedIn conversations started | 4 |
| Partner meetings | 1 |
| Discovery calls booked | 2 |
| Group answers posted | 8 |

Two discovery calls a week is around 100 a year. Close half and that is a client a fortnight; close
a third and it is one every three weeks. Either number gets you comfortably past the point where the
content starts carrying it. It is not a big number. It just has to happen every week, including the
bad ones.
