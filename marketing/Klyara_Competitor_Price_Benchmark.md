## Why this document exists

Klyara's tiers — Solo £59, Start £99, Trade £199 and Pro £329 a month, all plus VAT, with one-off
onboarding of £149, £250, from £350 and from £500 — were set before anybody checked what the market
actually charges. This document checks. It does three things:

1. Puts Klyara's prices next to real published UK prices for the same work.
2. Names who you are actually competing with in and around Guildford, and how they position.
3. Says, plainly, what to do about it.

Every third-party figure below came off a live page on **26 August 2026** and the URL is given.
Nothing here is a third-party price without a named source and that date. Two figures in this
document are **Klyara's own published comparison** rather than someone else's price list, and they
are labelled as such where they appear.

Prices move. **Re-run this in January 2027** — it is a two-hour job. Diarise it now for the first
week of January; the Day 90 review on Saturday 12 December 2026 is the last sensible reminder.

| Trade | £199 | Contractor-with-subbies bundle £300–£500 | **£101–£301/month under the direct comparator** |
| Pro | £329 | Multi-trade bundle £600–£1,500 | Roughly half the bottom of the range |

Three things fall out of that.

**One. Solo at £59 is priced below the cheapest single service inside it.** Solo does not file
monthly CIS returns — that is out of scope at this tier, see docs/21 — so the honest comparison is
not the CIS return. It is the bookkeeping, which starts at £100 a month in both general sources,
plus the two tax filings Solo includes: LOYALS charges £350 a year for a CIS subcontractor's self
assessment (£29 a month) and £150 a quarter for MTD for Income Tax (£50 a month), which is £79 a
month for the filings alone. Solo is £59 for the filings, the monthly bookkeeping, the software and
the CIS reconciliation. It is not a cheap tier; it is a loss leader, and it needs to be run as one
deliberately rather than accidentally.

**Two. Trade is the tier that is most obviously mispriced.** LOYALS publishes £300–£500 a month
for exactly the client Trade is aimed at — a contractor paying subcontractors. Trade is £199.
That is a discount of a third to three fifths against a firm that is not local, not in Surrey, and
by its own description charges more because it is chartered.

**Three. The comparison that sells this is the alternative, not the bundle.** The number now
published on the pricing page is the one to use everywhere: **your own evenings on the books at your
day rate, £640 a month · a high-street accountant doing it monthly, £395 a month · against Pro £329,
Trade £199, Solo £59.** The headline gap is **£441 a month** — £640 against Trade at £199.

Those two comparison figures are **Klyara's own published comparison, not a third-party price
list.** The £395 sits sensibly inside the published market above; the £640 is a day-rate assumption
and the hours and rate behind it need writing down once and holding to. Say the assumption out loud
when you use it: it is stronger, not weaker, for being shown.

The third-party bundle comparison still stands alongside it and is sourced: LOYALS publishes
£300–£500 a month for a contractor with subcontractors, so Trade at £199 is **£101–£301 a month
under the direct comparator**. Use the £441 on the page and the £101–£301 when someone names a rival
quote.

**Nobody is being told either number.** They belong on the pricing page, in the proposal, and in the
pricing minute of every discovery call.

## PART 3 — The recommendation

**Do not change the launch prices. Change the framing, and put a dated increase in the diary.**

The reasoning, in order.

Launching at £59–£329 with nothing to show — no reviews, no case studies, no track record, no
licence yet displayed — is defensible. Price is doing a job right now: it is buying the first ten
clients cheaply, and the first ten clients are worth more as proof than as revenue. Raising prices
before you have a single Google review would be trading the one advantage you currently hold.

But three things have to be true or the low price becomes permanent:

1. **The price is stated as an introductory rate from day one.** Not "our prices", but
   "founding client rate, held for twelve months from the date you join". The wording is in
   §Pricing language below. This costs nothing now and buys the entire increase later.
2. **The comparison number appears everywhere.** "Your own evenings at your day rate are £640 a
   month and a high-street accountant is £395. Trade is £199." Cheap without a reference point
   reads as low quality. Cheap against a stated £640 reads as a good deal from someone who knows
   what they are doing. Where a prospect wants a rival's price rather than the alternative to
   doing it at all, use the sourced £300–£500 contractor bundle instead.
3. **The increase is diarised now, not decided later.** Review on **1 September 2027**. Founding
   clients keep their rate to their twelve-month anniversary and then move to the standard rate,
   with sixty days' written notice. New clients from the review date pay the new rate.

### The four things this tells you

**Almost nobody publishes a price.** Of the nine firms above, one published a full price list and
it was the London one. Every local firm makes you fill in a form. That is the gap, and it is a large one.
A subcontractor at 7pm wants to know if this costs £80 or £800 before they give anyone their phone
number. Klyara publishing four tiers with numbers on them is a genuine competitive advantage and
should be pushed hard, not treated as a detail.

**"Construction specialist" is a claim, not a moat.** The nearest competitor uses the same words
Klyara does. The difference has to be visible in thirty seconds, and the honest differences are:
published prices, a named person who answers the phone, a Guildford address, and content that
demonstrates the specialism rather than asserting it. The twelve free calculators and the guide
library do more work here than any strapline.

**The nearest competitor is a national site with a town-name page.** That is beatable on local
signals — a real Google Business Profile with real reviews, a Guildford phone number, and pages
that mention Woking, Godalming, Farnham and Cranleigh because you actually work there. It is not
beatable on domain authority. Compete where you can win.

**Testimonials on a competitor's page are two names and five stars.** Klyara's homepage currently
carries invented testimonials and that has to be fixed before launch — it is flagged in docs/20 as
the highest-severity item and it is a banned practice under the DMCC Act 2024. The competitive
point is that the bar here is *low*: two real, named, verifiable reviews would already match the
firm currently ranking. There is no need for fifteen.

## PART 5 — Pricing language

Copy these. They are written to be said out loud.

**On the website, under the tiers**

> Founding client rate. Whatever you join on, that price is held for twelve months from your start
> date, plus a one-off onboarding fee quoted before you commit. Doing the books yourself in the
> evenings at your own day rate costs about £640 a month. A high-street accountant doing it monthly
> is about £395. Trade is £199.

**When someone says it seems cheap**

> It is cheap, and there is a reason rather than a catch. We do one industry, so we are not
> learning your scheme on your time — the CIS work that takes a general practice half a day takes
> us twenty minutes because we do forty of them a month. And we are new. You are getting a founding
> rate because your review is worth more to us right now than the difference in fee.

**When someone says another firm quoted less**

> Ask them two things. Is the CIS return in that price or billed per submission — £15 to £30 a time
> is normal. And is the software subscription in the fee or on top. Most of the cheap quotes in this
> trade are cheap because the CIS is extra, and CIS is the whole reason you are calling. Ask what
> the setup fee is, too — the published range for that is £200 to £800.

**When someone asks why it is not free like their bank's app**

> Because the app does not ring HMRC to verify a subcontractor, does not know that materials come
> out before the deduction, and does not file anything on the 19th. It records what happened. We
> are the bit that makes sure the right thing happened.

**At the twelve-month increase**

> Your founding rate runs to [date]. From [date + 60 days] you will move to the standard rate of
> £[x] a month plus VAT. Everything in the service stays the same. If that does not work, tell me
> now and we will look at the scope rather than have you leave over the difference.
