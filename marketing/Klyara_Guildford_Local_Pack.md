# 1. Why Guildford is a good place to launch this

Not flattery — these are the things that actually change the plan.

**The work is high-value domestic.** Guildford, Godalming, Farnham and the villages between them are
expensive housing stock: period property, conservation areas, and a large slice of the Surrey Hills
National Landscape where planning is tight. That produces extensions, loft conversions, basements,
barn conversions and full refurbishments rather than volume newbuild. Firms doing that work are the
5–15 person outfits Klyara is built for, and they invoice enough to be caught by MTD.

**High-value domestic work means end users.** Which means the VAT reverse charge question comes up
constantly and is answered wrongly constantly — a domestic client is an end user, the reverse charge
does not apply, and firms who work on both domestic and main-contractor jobs get this wrong in both
directions. That is a genuinely differentiating conversation to be able to have on a first call.

**Retentions bite hard here.** The commercial work in Guildford town, the university, the hospital
and the industrial estates runs on applications, certificates and retention. Your retention content
will land harder in Surrey than almost anywhere.

**There is real competition, and it isn't specialist.** Guildford has plenty of accountants. It has
very few construction-only bookkeepers. "We only do building firms" is a genuinely unusual claim in
this market, which is exactly why it works.

**Slyfield is the trade centre of gravity.** Selco is on Slyfield Industrial Estate, and Slyfield is
where the vans go. That concentrates your entire offline campaign into a couple of square miles.

**One caution, stated once:** Surrey is expensive, and £59–£329/month is going to read as cheap
rather than as a bargain to some of this market. Do not apologise for the price or discount it. The
value story is the specialism, not the saving.

```
A Surrey-specific problem that costs building firms real money.

Round here most firms run two kinds of work at once: high-value domestic — extensions, lofts,
basements, refurbs on period property — and jobs for main contractors.

Those two are treated completely differently for VAT, and firms get them wrong in both
directions.

DOMESTIC CLIENT
A homeowner is an end user. The reverse charge does NOT apply. You charge VAT as normal.

MAIN CONTRACTOR WHO'LL INVOICE YOUR WORK ONWARD
Not an end user. The reverse charge DOES apply. No VAT on the invoice.

Get the first one wrong and you've not charged VAT you should have — and you'll owe it to HMRC
out of your own pocket unless you can go back and charge it.

Get the second one wrong and you've collected VAT that isn't yours, which you'll be handing
over anyway, having overcharged a customer you'd like to work for again.

The bit almost nobody does: the customer has to tell you in writing that they're an end user.
It's their declaration, not your guess. Get it in an email at the start of the job and keep it.
```
