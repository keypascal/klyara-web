# 21 — Pricing, scope and quoting
### What is in each tier, what is not, what you charge for it, and how to say no without losing the client.

---

## Why this document exists

Every fixed-fee firm dies the same way. Not from bad pricing — from undefined scope. The fee is
agreed once and the work grows quietly for eighteen months until a £199 client is taking six hours
a month and you resent them. By then the conversation is impossible, because nothing was ever
written down that says where the line was.

This document draws the line. It is written to be used three ways: as your own reference when
quoting, as the scope schedule attached to every engagement letter, and as the script when
something falls outside it.

Benchmarking against real market prices is in **docs/22**. This document is about scope, not level.

---

# PART 1 — The four tiers, precisely

Prices are per month, **excluding VAT**, on a founding client rate held for twelve months from the
client's start date. Billed monthly in advance by Direct Debit or standing order. Pay a year up
front by Direct Debit and it is eleven months' fee for twelve.

**Every tier also carries a one-off onboarding fee.** It is published on the pricing page, it is
quoted exactly before the client commits, and it is not a deposit against the monthly. It covers the
same five things at every level: the backlog entered and reconciled to the bank, the software
configured in the client's own name, HMRC agent authorisation obtained, the portal issued, and the
receipt app demonstrated. It is sized to the backlog, so a client who arrives four months behind
pays more than one who arrives clean. **Live within five working days of signing**, bar HMRC's post.

| Tier | Monthly | Onboarding, one-off |
|---|---|---|
| Solo | £59 | £149 |
| Start | £99 | £250 |
| Trade | £199 | from £350 |
| Pro | £329 | from £500 |

All ex VAT. Quote the onboarding figure in the same breath as the monthly, always with its reason.
An unexplained onboarding fee is the most objected-to number in the whole price list; an explained
one is barely questioned.

## Solo — £59 a month, £149 onboarding

**Who it is for.** A sole-trader subcontractor. CIS suffered, not deducted. No employees. Under
the VAT threshold, or newly over it and not yet registered. Typically 20–60 transactions a month.

**In scope**

- Bank feed reconciled monthly
- Sales invoices raised from your job sheet or WhatsApp, up to **10 per month**
- Purchase receipts captured and coded, up to **50 items per month**
- CIS suffered recorded and reconciled to the statements you are given
- Chasing missing CIS statements from your contractors, up to **3 chases per month**
- Self Assessment prepared and filed, once a year, including the CIS reclaim
- Software subscription included, in your name
- Monthly one-page summary: what you invoiced, what you were paid, what CIS was taken
- Email and WhatsApp support, answered within one working day

**Onboarding — £149 one-off.** Fixed, not "from". The current tax year to date entered and
reconciled, software set up in your name, HMRC agent authorisation, portal login, receipt app
demonstrated. Prior years are out of scope at this tier — they are catch-up at £45 an hour, quoted
after a look and capped at the quote.

**Out of scope** — VAT registration or returns · payroll of any kind · limited company accounts or
CT600 · acting as a contractor and filing monthly returns · management accounts · mortgage
references beyond issuing the SA302 · catch-up of prior years

**The natural upgrade trigger.** They take on their first subcontractor, or they register for VAT.
Both move them to Start at minimum.

## Start — £99 a month, £250 onboarding

**Who it is for.** A sole trader or single-director limited company, VAT registered, still working
alone or with one hand. Typically 60–150 transactions a month.

**In scope — everything in Solo, plus**

- VAT returns prepared, reverse charge applied correctly in both directions, and filed
- Sales invoices raised, up to **25 per month**
- Purchase items, up to **120 per month**
- Director-only payroll, if a limited company — one payslip a month, RTI filed
- Limited company year-end accounts and CT600, **or** Self Assessment for a sole trader
- Confirmation statement filed
- Quarterly fifteen-minute call, if you want it

**Onboarding — £250 one-off.** Fixed, not "from". Everything in Solo's onboarding, plus the VAT
registration details checked and the reverse charge position set up correctly from day one, and the
payroll scheme picked up if there is one.

**Out of scope** — filing monthly CIS returns as a contractor · payroll beyond the director ·
CIS verification of subcontractors · management accounts · budgeting and forecasting · catch-up of
prior years

**The natural upgrade trigger.** The first subcontractor they pay. That is Trade, immediately —
not next month, not at the year end.

---

## Trade — £199 a month, onboarding from £350

**Who it is for.** The core client. A contractor paying subcontractors, VAT registered, one to
five people. This is the tier the whole business is built around and it should be roughly 60% of
the book. Typically 150–350 transactions a month.

**In scope — everything in Start, plus**

- Subcontractor verification with HMRC — **unlimited**
- Monthly CIS returns prepared and **filed by the 19th**, every month, including nil returns
- Subcontractor payment and deduction statements issued by the 19th, up to **10 subcontractors**
- Materials and labour split checked on every subcontractor invoice
- Payroll for up to **5 employees**, RTI, and the pension submission
- Sales invoices raised, up to **60 per month**
- Purchase items, up to **300 per month**
- Retentions tracked on a separate schedule and reported monthly
- Reverse charge handled on sales and purchases, with the end user declarations chased and filed
- Debtor list issued monthly with a suggested chase order
- Monthly management summary: turnover, gross margin, CIS position, retentions outstanding, cash
- Monthly fifteen-minute call

**Onboarding — from £350 one-off.** Quoted exactly before you commit, sized on the backlog:
everything in Start's onboarding, plus subcontractors loaded and verified with HMRC, the CIS
position reconciled to the statements you hold, and any retention schedule opened. £350 assumes up
to **6 months** of the current year's backlog and up to **10 subcontractors**; beyond either, it is
quoted on the count. Prior years are catch-up at £45 an hour, quoted separately.

**Out of scope** — CITB levy returns · construction contract review · job costing beyond the
monthly split · more than 5 employees · more than 10 subcontractors · catch-up of prior years ·
tax planning beyond the year-end return · anything requiring a site visit

**The natural upgrade trigger.** Six employees, eleven subcontractors, or the first month they ask
for a job-by-job profit figure.

---

## Pro — £329 a month, onboarding from £500

**Who it is for.** An established firm, £500k–£2m turnover, running multiple jobs at once and
needing to know which ones make money. Typically 350–700 transactions a month.

**In scope — everything in Trade, plus**

- Payroll for up to **15 employees**
- Up to **25 subcontractors** verified, deducted, and statemented
- Sales invoices raised, up to **150 per month**, then £1.50 each on the rate card
- Purchase items, up to **700 per month**
- **Job costing** — cost and margin tracked per job, reported monthly
- Applications for payment and valuations recorded against the retention schedule
- CIS suffered and deducted both reconciled — for firms in the middle of the chain
- Management accounts with a short written commentary each month
- Cash flow forecast, rolled forward quarterly
- Monthly thirty-minute call
- Same-day response on anything urgent, in working hours (Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm)

**Onboarding — from £500 one-off.** Quoted exactly before you commit. Everything in Trade's
onboarding, plus the job list built and opened, opening balances agreed with the outgoing
accountant, and the first month run in parallel to prove the figures.

**Out of scope** — CITB levy returns · audit · corporate finance · contract or legal review ·
site attendance · anything a chartered accountant is properly required for

**Above Pro.** Do not invent a tier. Quote it, using PART 3.

# PART 2 — The rate card for everything else

Publish this. Firms that hide it end up doing the work for free.

| Work | Price |
|---|---|
| Gross payment status application, prepared and submitted | £195 (free on Trade and Pro) |
| Reverse charge setup and supplier/customer review | £125 (free at onboarding) |
| Catch-up of prior-period books | £45/hour, quoted after a look, capped at the quote |
| Additional subcontractor beyond the tier limit | £8/month each |
| Additional employee on payroll beyond the tier limit | £6/month each |
| Extra sales invoices beyond the tier limit | £1.50 each |
| CIS return for a month we did not do the bookkeeping | £45 |
| Missed-return catch-up filing, per return | £35 |
| Mortgage or lending pack beyond the SA302 | £75 |
| Reference letter to a lender or main contractor | £45 |
| Ad hoc management report, one-off | £120 |
| Company formation | £150, or referred out |
| VAT registration | £95 |
| CIS registration as contractor or subcontractor | £65 |
| HMRC enquiry support | £65/hour |
| Software migration from another package | £195 |
| Out-of-scope work, general | £45/hour, billed in 15-minute units |
| Records in after the 10th but still wanted in that cycle | £25 surcharge |

All ex VAT. **Records in by the 10th are guaranteed into that month's cycle.** Later than the 10th
and the work rolls to the next cycle, or stays in this one with the £25 surcharge. Say which at the
time, in writing.

**Two rules about this card.**

It exists so you can say yes cheerfully. "Yes, absolutely — that one is outside the monthly, it is
£75, want me to go ahead?" is a completely normal sentence and clients hear it as competence.

And nothing on it gets billed without being agreed first, in writing, before the work starts. A
surprise invoice costs more goodwill than the fee is worth.

# PART 3 — Quoting a non-standard job

For anything that does not fit a tier. Do this on paper before you say a number.

**Step 1 — Count the volume.** Ask for three months of bank statements and count the lines. Not a
guess. Transactions per month is the single best predictor of your time.

**Step 2 — Find the tier it lands in by volume.**

| Transactions/month | Base tier |
|---|---|
| Under 60 | Solo |
| 60–150 | Start |
| 150–350 | Trade |
| 350–700 | Pro |
| 700+ | Pro plus, use step 4 |

**Step 3 — Apply the complexity multipliers.** These are additive on the base fee.

| Factor | Add |
|---|---|
| Acts as a contractor as well as a subcontractor | +20% |
| More than 10 subcontractors | +£8 each above 10 |
| More than the tier's employees | +£6 each above the limit |
| Job costing required | +25% |
| Retentions on more than 5 live jobs | +10% |
| Two or more trading entities | +60% of the smaller entity's tier |
| Books currently in a carrier bag | Catch-up quoted separately, not folded in |
| Records arrive after the 10th in two of any three months | +15%, or decline |
| More than one bank account or a credit card | +5% each |

**Step 4 — Sanity check against your own time.** Divide the fee by the hours you think it will
take. If it is below **£38/hour** you have underquoted. Below £30, walk away. Your target across
the book is £45–£60 an hour of your time; the rate card above is set at £45 for a reason.

**Step 5 — Round up, never down.** £284 becomes £295. Never quote an odd number you cannot defend.

**Step 6 — Quote a review date.** "That is £295 a month. I will review it with you at ninety days
and again at twelve months, and if the volume is not what we thought I will tell you either way."
This single sentence is what makes a fixed fee survivable, because it makes going *up* a normal
event rather than a confrontation.

# PART 4 — Scope creep, and what to say

Scope creep is not a client problem. It is a documentation problem, and it always happens the same
way: a small favour, granted twice, becomes an expectation.

## The three-strike rule

**First time.** Do it. Say nothing about the fee. It is goodwill and it is cheap.

**Second time.** Do it, and name it.

> "Happy to — done. Just so you know for next time, that one sits outside the monthly. It is the
> sort of thing I would normally pick up at £45 an hour. Not charging you for this one, but if it
> becomes a regular thing let's build it into the fee properly."

**Third time.** Do not do it silently.

> "This is the third of these, so it has stopped being a one-off. Two options. I can add it to the
> monthly — it works out about £30 — or I bill it as it comes at £45 an hour. The monthly is
> cheaper if it keeps happening. Which suits?"

You have now had a fee increase conversation without it being a fee increase conversation.

## The phrases that hold the line

**When they ask for something outside the tier**

> "Yes, I can do that. It is outside the monthly so it is £[x] — want me to crack on?"

**When they push back on being charged**

> "The monthly fee covers a defined list, and it is fixed precisely so it does not creep up on you.
> Anything outside it, I tell you the price before I start. You will never get a surprise invoice
> from me — but you will occasionally get a quote."

**When the volume has clearly grown**

> "When we set the fee you were doing about 90 transactions a month. Last three months have been
> 240 and you have taken on two subbies, which is great. That is genuinely a Trade-sized job now.
> I would move you to £199 from [date, at least 30 days out]. You are getting more back for it —
> the CIS returns and the payroll come in at that level."

**When they want a discount for paying annually**

> "Sure — twelve months up front is eleven months' fee. It helps my cash flow and it should help
> your planning."

That is an 8.3% discount for a year of cash up front. Offer it. Do not offer more.

**When they simply cannot afford the right tier**

> "Then let's do less, not the same for less. I will do [reduced scope] at £[lower tier] and you
> keep doing [x] yourself. When it gets too much, we move you up. I would rather do a smaller job
> properly than a big one badly."

Never discount the same scope. The moment you do, the price becomes the opening bid for everyone
they talk to on site.

# PART 5 — Getting paid

You are a bookkeeper. Being bad at your own credit control is a positioning problem as well as a
cash problem.

**Terms.** Monthly in advance, Direct Debit preferred, standing order accepted, on the 1st.
Invoice issued on the 25th of the preceding month.

**Set up Direct Debit from day one.** GoCardless or the equivalent inside your accounting software.
Clients on Direct Debit pay on time roughly universally; clients on standing order forget when
the fee changes; clients on bank transfer pay when they feel like it. Make Direct Debit the
default on the engagement letter and standing order the exception.

**If the payment fails**

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Automatic retry, no contact |
| Day 3 | Friendly text. "Direct debit bounced — worth a check?" |
| Day 7 | Phone call. Nothing else works as well. |
| Day 14 | Written notice: work pauses at day 21 if unresolved |
| Day 21 | Work pauses. Filing deadlines within 14 days are still met — see below. |
| Day 30 | Formal notice, engagement ends at day 60 |

**The exception you must honour.** If a statutory deadline falls in the pause window and the work
is already done, file it. You do not create a penalty for a client over a fee dispute, and you do
not want that on your record with your professional body. File it, tell them you filed it, and
tell them the pause continues.

**Never hold the records hostage.** Their data is theirs. The software subscription is in their
name specifically so that this is never a lever, and that fact is a selling point on the way in —
it should not become a weapon on the way out. Chase the fee as a debt, properly and separately.

**Price increases, annually, in writing, sixty days ahead.** Not "as required". Every client, same
month, every year. A predictable small increase is accepted; an unpredictable large one is why
people leave. The founding rate schedule and the September 2027 review are in docs/22.

# PART 6 — The annual increase letter

Send by email, 60 days before the effective date. Change the bracketed parts and nothing else.

> **Subject: Your fee from [1 November]**
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Quick note about your monthly fee, well ahead of it changing so there are no surprises.
>
> You joined on our founding client rate of £[199] a month. That rate was held for twelve months
> and it comes to an end on [31 October]. From **[1 November]** your fee will be **£[279] a month
> plus VAT**.
>
> Nothing about the service changes. Same work, same deadlines, same person.
>
> Two things worth saying plainly. First, this is the first increase since you joined — the founding
> rate did what it was meant to do and it held for the full year. Second, £[279] is still well under
> what this work costs any other way: a high-street practice doing it monthly is about £395, and
> doing it yourself in the evenings at your own day rate is about £640.
>
> If the shape of your business has changed and the tier is no longer right, tell me and we will
> look at it properly rather than have you pay for something you do not need.
>
> Your direct debit will update automatically on [1 November]. Nothing for you to do.
>
> [Name]
> Klyara

**Do not apologise in this letter.** No "unfortunately", no "due to rising costs", no "we have had
to". You are stating a price. Confident and brief gets accepted; apologetic and long invites a
negotiation.

# PART 7 — The numbers to hold yourself to

Review these monthly against the KPI workbook.

| Metric | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Average revenue per client | £160+ by month 12 | Weighted to Trade, not Solo |
| Effective hourly rate across the book | £45–£60 | Below £38 on any client, requote it |
| Proportion on Trade or Pro | 60%+ | Where the business actually is |
| Clients on Direct Debit | 90%+ | Everything else is unpaid admin |
| Debtor days | Under 5 | You bill in advance; there is no excuse |
| Out-of-scope work billed vs done | 80%+ | The single biggest silent leak |
| Clients on a founding rate past 12 months | 0 | The whole point of dating it |
| Fee increases accepted without loss | 90%+ | Below that, the increase was a surprise |

**The one to watch hardest is the sixth.** Out-of-scope work that gets done and not billed is
invisible in every report — it does not show up as a loss, it shows up as you being tired. Keep a
running note in the client file every time you do something off the rate card, billed or not. At
the ninety-day review you will be able to see the pattern and price it in.
